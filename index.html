What the chip does differently

Protocols are programs, and every edge sits on a clock grid. Two barrel cores of two hardware threads (harts) each drive eight protocol pins. An instruction is a pin operation: drive a level, shift a bit, wait for a level or for an exact time. Each hart runs one instruction every 40 ns whatever the other harts do, and a period timer places edges at exact multiples of a period however long the code between them took. The program memories are SRAM loaded over SPI after fabrication, so a new protocol is a new file: the repository holds 154 firmware files, 110 of them added while the RTL stayed unchanged.

From a device's place, the chip can measure the host. A predictive edge launcher learns the clock of the other side and puts the chip's reply edge before, on or after the predicted clock edge, to one clock (20 ns). Stepping that offset finds both ends of the host's sampling window. On FPGA boards this ran against an unmodified vendor SPI host (Xilinx AXI Quad SPI under Linux) and matched a prediction committed before the run; that run used an older RTL and is not repeated on the submitted one (details and limits).

On the board, against real devices

The RTL of the submitted chip without its shift unit, on a ZCU104 FPGA at 50 MHz, met four real devices while the Vivado ILA recorded its pads. Each device's own tool shows what it received. SWD: DPIDR 0x2BA01477, CPUID 0x410FC241, DBGMCU_IDCODE 0x20036469 during the ILA capture, STM32CubeProgrammer reads 0x20036469 at 0xE0042000; USB: 10 of 10 runs configured, kernel 'New USB device found 1209:0001'; CAN 500 kbit/s: chip to KD240 4 of 4 acknowledged, KD240 to chip 3 of 3, TEC, REC and overruns 0; 10BASE-T: 20 frames sent, 20 in the PC's capture.

On 2026-11-27 the RTL of int9_line ran on board 1 with two signals moved off damaged pins: S1 read right, a PC enumerated USB full speed on the dedicated USB unit 5 of 5, G5-a on the Protocol Block agreed with its prediction; the board stopped answering after three items and the rest ran after a power cycle, where the F446 SWD host did not pass (board page).
