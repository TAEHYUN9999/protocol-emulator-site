File Role Words
firmware/pdm.pe Hart 0 is a PDM receiver: it drives CLK (1.136 MHz by default), reads DATA at both edges (left channel at the falling edge, right at the rising edge) and, every R clock periods, reports the number of 1 bits of each channel (a first-order CIC, integrate and dump) to the host as one PCM pair. Hart 1 is a PDM microphone: a first-order sigma-delta modulator per channel driving DATA on the edges of whatever CLK it sees, left, right or both. With both harts the chip is a loopback; each hart also runs alone against an outside microphone or codec. 85 of 512 (hart 1 from word 41)

No RTL change. Checked in simulation only.

Channel edges secondary, two public sources that agree: TDK INMP621 data sheet ("the left channel is clocked on the falling edge of CLK, and the right channel is clocked on the rising edge"), Nordic nRF52 PDM description ("bits from the left PDM microphone are sampled on PDM_CLK falling edge, bits for the right are sampled on the rising edge") a left microphone drives after the rising edge and lets go after the falling edge, a right one the other way round
First-order CIC and sigma-delta modulation textbook definitions the decimator (sum of R bits) and the modulator (acc += level, carry = bit)

Pins and settings

Pin Use
P0 CLK: driven by hart 0; read by hart 1
P1 DATA: read by hart 0; driven by hart 1 (released when it does not drive)
Data memory (DATA_W before the run) Use Default (0)
0x00 HALF ticks per clock half; at least 10 11: 1.136 MHz
0x01 R clock periods per PCM pair 64: fs = 17.76 kHz
0x02 SEL hart 1: 0 left, 1 right, 2 both 0
0x03, 0x04 hart 1: left and right levels (bit density = level / 256) 0

The PCM pairs go to the host FIFO with txb.nb: the host must read them in time (2 bytes every R x 2 HALF ticks), a full FIFO drops them.

Timing

Hart 0 runs on a twait grid of HALF ticks: each half holds the edge, the read of DATA (the synchronized level, i.e. the level just before the edge) and the count; at the end of a window the counts are kept and sent over the next two halves, so no half holds more than 9 instructions and HALF = 10 (1.25 MHz) is the shortest. 1.136 MHz is inside the 1.05-1.9 MHz range; the round 1.0 MHz (12.5 ticks a half) is not a whole number of ticks, and 12 ticks (1.042 MHz) falls between two IM69D130 ranges.

Hart 1 waits for each edge with waitp and then drives the bit (or lets go), a few ticks after the edge.

Tests

test/test_pdm.py, run from a private directory:

T=<repo>/test; R=$T/sim_build/<name>; mkdir -p $R; cd $R
PYTHONPATH=$T make -B -f $T/Makefile PWD=$T SIM_BUILD=$R/build COCOTB_TEST_MODULES=test_pdm

Mic is two microphone models with the IM69D130 times (drive 60 ns after the edge, release 20 ns after the other one) and their own sigma-delta modulators; Codec drives CLK and takes DATA before each edge; the expected PCM values are the 1-bit counts of the model's bits in each window of R periods from the first falling edge.

Limits

  • A first-order CIC only (R up to 255 periods, counts up to R): no further filtering or droop correction; the PCM value is the raw count.
  • The emulated microphone is slower than the part: it drives DATA up to about 220 ns after the edge (tDD 80 ns) and lets go up to about 140 ns after (tHZ 30 ns), so two emulated microphones on one DATA line would overlap; one hart driving both channels (SEL 2) does not release between them.
  • The receiver's CLK is at most 1.25 MHz.
