File Role Words
firmware/snes.pe Hart 0: an NES or SNES controller (the CD4021B shift register: loaded while LATCH is high, one bit per CLK rise, 0s after the report). Hart 1: the console side (12 us latch, 8 or 16 clocks of 12 us, data taken at each CLK fall). 64 of 512 (hart 1 starts at word 34)
firmware/joybus.pe Hart 0: an N64 controller on the one-wire joybus (info 00h and FFh, state 01h, 1 us units, its stop bit). Hart 1: a console that sends host commands and reads the replies. 124 of 512 (hart 1 starts at word 59)

These protocols were not part of the design when the RTL was written. They use only existing instructions (shin as a pin test, shin.lsb, shout.lsb, shout.od, low/high/rel, waitp, waitp.to, tset/twait, the host FIFO and the data memory); the RTL is unchanged. Each file's two harts can face each other on one chip. Checked in simulation only, not on a board.

NES and SNES

The pad is two CD4021B parallel-in shift registers in series (one for the NES). Their behaviour is from the TI CD4021B data sheet (SCHS024C, original): while PARALLEL/SERIAL CONTROL is high the parallel inputs load asynchronously and Q8 shows P8; while it is low each CLOCK rise shifts one stage and Q1 takes the serial input; a CLOCK fall changes nothing (truth table, Fig. 2). Nintendo published no controller specification; button order, levels and port timing come from secondary sources:

Item Source Firmware
NES report A, B, Select, Start, Up, Down, Left, Right; an unpressed button stored as 1 (the console inverts); after 8 bits a standard pad reads 1 at the CPU, so 0 on the wire; CLK idles high and the 4021 shifts on its rise nesdev.org "Standard controller" pad pad..pw2: wire = not pressed, then the grounded serial input
SNES report B, Y, Select, Start, Up, Down, Left, Right, A, X, L, R, then four bits read as 0 (wire 1), then 1s (wire 0) nesdev.org "SNES controller" BTN1 bits 4..7 are 0
A 12 us positive latch pulse, 6 us later clock pulses of 12 us at 50 % duty; the pad shifts on each rise, the console samples on each fall J. Christy, SNES pinouts and protocol FAQ, as quoted by RepairFAQ console: LATCH 12 us, 6 us, 6 us low / 6 us high

Pins: P0 LATCH, P1 CLK (console outputs), P2 DATA (pad output, push-pull). Settings: 0x00 MODE (0 NES, 1 SNES), 0x01 and 0x02 the pad buttons, 1 = pressed, bit 0 first; 0x03 counts latch pulses. The console reads on any host byte and answers the report bytes, 1 = pressed.

N64 joybus

Nintendo published no joybus specification. The timing and commands come from two secondary sources that agree: the n64brew.dev "Joybus Protocol" page and raphnet's gc_n64_usb-v3 sources (gcn64txrx.S, n64.c).

Item Source Firmware
Bits of 4 us: a 0 is 3 us low then 1 us high, a 1 is 1 us low then 3 us high, MSB first n64brew text and "Joybus Bits" diagram, raphnet gcn64txrx.S (3 us / 1 us diagram, mask 80h first) 1 us slots: low, the bit twice, high (shout.od)
Console stop bit 3 us (1 us low), controller stop bit 4 us (2 us low) n64brew text and its "Joybus Bits" diagram (1 us grid) console 1 slot low; controller 2 slots low
The line idles high; a device does not drive it high (raphnet sends a 1 by making the pin an input) n64brew, raphnet open drain on P3
FFh reset and info, 00h info: 05h 00h and the accessory status (02h nothing in the slot); 01h state: 4 bytes, A B Z Start Up Down Left Right, RST, 0, L R C-Up C-Down C-Left C-Right, then X and Y in two's complement; FFh resets the stick to (0, 0); unsupported commands get no reply n64brew (info, status byte, RST, stick), raphnet n64.c (bit order) controller ci, cr, cs

The controller samples each command bit 2 us after its fall and starts its reply 2 us after the console stop bit; one-byte commands only. The console sends a host byte as a command, reads 3 bytes for 00h and FFh, 4 for 01h and none for others, checks the controller stop bit, and answers the host [status, bytes]: 0 ok, 1 no reply within 100 us of a bit, 2 no stop bit. Settings: 0x00..0x03 the controller state (two button bytes, X, Y), 0x07 the accessory status (0: 02h), 0x04 counts answered commands.

Controls

Each change was made in a copy of snes.pe or joybus.pe and run with SNES_FW or JOY_FW pointing at it. All fail, each at the check it aims at:

Change Test Caught by
Pad shifts on the CLK fall instead of the rise test_pad_nes_snes NES report wrong (bits one clock late)
Pad drives a pressed button high (no inversion) test_pad_nes_snes NES report wrong
Console half period 4 us instead of 6 us test_pad_console latch 400 clocks (8 us; 12 us expected)
Controller samples 0.6 us after the fall instead of 2 us test_joy_controller 01h taken as 00h: 25 reply pulses instead of 33
Controller stop bit without its 2 us low test_joy_controller stop bit 50 clocks low (100 expected)
Console stop bit 2 us low instead of 1 us test_joy_console stop bit 100 clocks low (50 expected)

Limits

  • No NES Four Score, SNES mouse or multitap; the console reads once per host byte, not every 16.7 ms.
  • The pad's DATA follows a CLK rise within 0.52 us (the console samples 6 us later); a console clocking faster than about 1 MHz would need more.
  • Joybus: no Controller Pak (02h, 03h), no GameCube commands, no multi-byte commands; the controller answers 2 us after the stop bit (real controllers take a few us more); the sample point at 2 us accepts bit lows from below 2 us (1) and above 2 us (0) only.
  • Simulation only: no board, no gate-level run.
