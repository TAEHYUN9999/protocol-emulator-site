File Role Words
firmware/mdio.pe MDIO station management entity (STA) on hart 0 and PHY on hart 1, on the same two pins. The STA sends read and write frames for the host and reports a PHY that does not answer. The PHY answers frames for its address from a file of 32 registers in the data memory. 210 of 512 (hart 1 starts at word 117)

This protocol was not part of the design when the RTL was written. It uses only existing instructions (low/high/rel, shout, shin, waitp, tset/tsetr/twait, ld/st, host FIFO); the RTL is unchanged. Checked in simulation only, not on a board.

Source

The original text of IEEE 802.3 Clause 22 (22.2.2.13 MDC, 22.2.2.14 MDIO, 22.2.4.5 management frame structure, 22.3.4 MDIO timing) was not available: the standard is not public, and the public IEEE 802 documents found by search did not quote these subclauses. The models and the firmware follow the Clause 22 management interface as one PHY datasheet describes it:

  • Texas Instruments, DP83848C/I/VYB/YB PHYTER datasheet, SNLS266E (May 2007, revised March 2015): 6.4.3.2 Serial Management Access Protocol, Table 6-4, Figures 6-2 and 6-3, 6.4.3.3 Preamble Suppression, and the MII serial management timing table (T2.3.1 to T2.3.4).

The Clause 22 figures that are often quoted (MDC period at least 400 ns, MDC high and low at least 160 ns, PHY output delay up to 300 ns) were not checked against the standard and are not used as pass criteria.

Pins

Pin Use
uio[2] (P2) MDC: driven by the STA (low between frames), read by the PHY
uio[3] (P3) MDIO, three-state: driven by the STA or the PHY, released otherwise. Needs a pull-up.

Both harts use the same two pins, so the chip is an STA and a PHY on one management bus. pin_out and pin_oe in src/pe_core.v are shared, but every pin instruction changes only the bit of the pin it names, and only one side drives MDIO at a time: the STA releases MDIO once, at the start of the first turnaround bit of a read, and does not touch it again until its next frame; the PHY drives only from the second turnaround bit through data bit 0 of a read for its own address. MDC is written by the STA alone. Other PHYs and STAs (the test models) share the same wires outside the chip.

Timing

One tick is 40 ns.

STA: MDC comes from a twait grid of HALF ticks (data memory 0x01). For bits the STA sends, MDC is low and high HALF ticks each and MDIO changes one tick after the falling edge. For bits it reads, the sample takes the tick before the rise, so MDC is low HALF + 1 and high HALF - 1 ticks, and MDIO is sampled at the end of the low phase. HALF = 5 gives 2.5 MHz.

PHY: each bit is read one tick after the rising edge is seen (waitp MDC, 1 then shin). On a read the PHY changes MDIO one tick after it sees a rising edge.

HALF 5 (2.5 MHz, the DP83848C's typical MDC) is the fastest setting that keeps the twait grid (late flag 0): at HALF 4 and 3 the few instructions at a field boundary (loop count, call/ret) take a whole half period, the twait comes late and that MDC phase is stretched, which a PHY does not mind; the frames still pass. At 250 ns of PHY delay the STA samples before the PHY's bit has arrived once the period is 320 ns or less. MDIO driven by the STA at HALF 5: setup at least 160 ns and hold at least 240 ns around every rising edge (test_mdio_sta; DP83848C T2.3.2/T2.3.3 ask 10 ns each).

Fastest MDC for the PHY: 3.57 MHz (tested steps). The PHY reads MDIO one tick after it sees the rising edge, behind the two synchronizer flip-flops, so it needs MDIO held 80 ns after the rising edge; an STA that changes MDIO at the falling edge, as the STA model and the chip's own STA do, holds it for half a period. The DP83848C asks only 10 ns of hold (T2.3.3); an STA that changes MDIO 40 to 70 ns after the rise failed here, and shorter holds were not tried. The PHY's MDIO output changes 100 to 120 ns after the rising edge (all read frames of test_mdio_phy; 100 ns in the loopback), against 0 to 30 ns for the DP83848C (T2.3.1).

Host interface and settings

Settings (data memory, DATA_W before the run)

Address Name Use
0x00 PHYAD Address of the PHY on hart 1, 0 to 31
0x01 HALF MDC half period of the STA on hart 0, in ticks (5: 2.5 MHz; 4 and below set the late flag, see Timing)
0xA0 + r PHY register r, bits 15..8
0xC0 + r PHY register r, bits 7..0

The registers are plain storage: the host presets them with DATA_W and reads them with DATA_R at any time; a write frame changes them. There are no read-only bits and no side effects. Data memory 0x02 to 0x1F, 0xE0 to 0xFF is not used.

STA (hart 0)

Direction Bytes
Host to chip 1 phy reg: read; 2 phy reg hi lo: write. Any other first byte is dropped.
Chip to host read: status hi lo; write: status

Status 0: done. Status 1: no PHY answered: the second turnaround bit of the read was 1 (the pull-up), and the data is what the line showed, FFFFh. A write cannot be checked on the bus (nothing comes back), so its status is always 0. The STA takes the whole command from the FIFO before the preamble, so the MDC timing does not depend on the host.

PHY (hart 1)

The PHY reads PHYAD once at the start. It answers reads and takes writes for that address only. Address 0 has no special meaning.

The register address costs no instruction: the five REGAD bits are shifted into a register that starts at 5, so after five shifts it holds 0xA0 + REGAD, the address of the high byte.

Tests

test/test_mdio.py (cocotb). The models are written from the DP83848C description of the Clause 22 interface (see Source), not from the firmware: a bus recorder that decodes frames the way a PHY without preamble suppression reads them (32 or more ones, then start, op, addresses, turnaround, data, each bit the level just before a rising edge of MDC) and records who drives MDIO at every bit, the chip (uio_oe) or a model; a PHY model with a register file and a settable output delay; an STA model with settable MDC high and low times, preamble length, turnaround, idle cycles and the point where it changes MDIO. A conflict (the chip and a model driving MDIO at once) fails every test. With drive=False the PHY model only predicts which frames a correct PHY takes; test_mdio_phy compares the chip with that prediction. Only the FIFO bytes and the data memory addresses come from this document. Run from a private directory:

T=<repo>/test; R=$T/sim_build/<name>; mkdir -p $R; cd $R
PYTHONPATH=$T make -B -f $T/Makefile PWD=$T SIM_BUILD=$R/build COCOTB_TEST_MODULES=test_mdio

One run of the whole module on 2026-11-11: TESTS=5 PASS=5 FAIL=0, 81.4 ms of simulated time, 883 s of wall time on a loaded machine (COCOTB_TEST_FILTER runs one test).

Limits

  • PHY input hold: MDIO must stay 80 ns after the rising edge of MDC (DP83848C asks 10 ns). STAs that change MDIO at the falling edge give half a period.
  • PHY output delay 100 to 120 ns after the rising edge (DP83848C 0 to 30 ns); the STA then has the rest of the period to sample.
  • After data bit 0 of a write the PHY spends 8 ticks storing the register before it counts preamble ones again (counted from the code, not measured). With an MDC period of 8 ticks or less (3.1 MHz and above) a frame that follows at once could lose one preamble one; the tests leave MDC idle after a write. The DP83848C text (6.4.3.3) says one idle bit between transactions is required.
  • No preamble suppression: every frame needs 32 ones (DP83848C supports suppression, 6.4.3.3; it is optional).
  • Address 0 is an ordinary address: no broadcast and no isolate meaning (on the DP83848C, strapping address 0 selects MII isolate mode, 6.4.5.1).
  • Ones before a 31-one preamble: the PHY starts counting ones after the end of the previous frame (its own or one for another address), so data bits of an earlier frame never count as preamble. Whether a real PHY counts data bits of a frame for another address is not stated in the DP83848C text; the tests only use 31-one preambles that follow a 0 or the start.
  • The registers have no bit behaviour (reset bit 0.15, read-only status bits, latched link status); they are memory for the host.
  • The STA reports a missing PHY on reads only; a write has nothing to check on the bus.
  • HALF must be 3 or more; HALF 3 and 4 work but set the late flag (stretched MDC phases at field boundaries).
  • Not tested: a real PHY, a board, gate-level simulation.
