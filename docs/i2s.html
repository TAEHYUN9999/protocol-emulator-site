File Role Words
firmware/i2s.pe I2S transmitter on hart 0, as the controller (drives SCK, WS and SD): plays a buffer of stereo 16-bit frames in a loop, word length 8 or 16 bits in slots of 4 to 255 bits. I2S receiver on hart 1, as a target (takes SCK and WS from outside): word length 8 or 16 bits from any slot length, stored in a ring in the data memory. Each runs alone, or both at once (a loopback over three wires). 298 of 512 (hart 1 starts at word 99)
firmware/i2s_frac.pe i2s.pe with the transmitter on the fractional tick generator (int6): exact 32, 44.1 and 48 kHz, see Exact rates with the tick generator. 305 of 512 (hart 1 starts at word 106)

Word counts are from tools/peasm.py. This protocol was not part of the design when the RTL was written. It uses only existing instructions (twait grid, low/high, shout for SD and for WS, waitp, shin, ld/st to the frame buffer and the data memory); the RTL is unchanged. Checked in simulation only, not on a board.

Source

NXP UM11732, "I2S bus specification", Rev. 3.0, 17 February 2022 (user manual, 14 pages, nxp.com/docs/en/user-manual/UM11732.pdf). Its revision history says it is reconstructed from the Philips Semiconductors I2S bus specification of February 1986, revised June 5, 1996, with the terms "master" and "slave" changed to "controller" and "target". The 1996 Philips text (7 pages) was compared: the section numbers and the text are the same; its single timing table "Table 1" is UM11732's Table 3, and its two 2.5 MHz examples are UM11732's Tables 1 and 2. References below are to UM11732.

Pins

Pin Use
uio[0] (P0) SCK out, push-pull (transmitter, controller)
uio[1] (P1) WS out, push-pull
uio[2] (P2) SD out, push-pull
uio[3] (P3) SCK in (receiver, target)
uio[4] (P4) WS in
uio[5] (P5) SD in

The pins are .equ values (SCK, WS, SD, RSCK, RWS, RSD). The receiver writes no pin, so the two harts never touch the same bit of pin_out/pin_oe. For a loopback on one chip wire P0 to P3, P1 to P4 and P2 to P5 (the test does this), or assemble with RSCK = 0, RWS = 1, RSD = 2: the receiver then reads the transmitter's pins through the pads.

Why this split: I2S needs one device to own SCK and WS. A controller transmitter and a target receiver are the two roles most devices take (a codec's DAC input is a target receiver, an ADC or a microphone is often a transmitter that a controller clocks), and with the receiver as a target each half can be tested alone against a model of the other, and together over three wires. A target transmitter or a controller receiver would need the same bit loops with the clock taken from or given to the other side; they are not implemented.

Settings and host interface

Data memory, written by the host with DATA_W before the run (both harts stopped) and read with DATA_R at any time. Each hart checks its settings once at start and writes back the values it uses.

Address Name Use 0 or out of range
0x00 HALF transmitter: SCK half period in ticks (40 ns), 5 to 255 0: 25 (SCK 500 kHz); 1 to 4: 5 (2.5 MHz)
0x01 SLOT transmitter: SCK periods per channel (the system word length), 4 to 255; a frame is 2 x SLOT periods 0: 16; 1 to 3: 4
0x02 TXW transmitter word length, 8 or 16: 8 sends the high byte of each sample, 16 both bytes; the rest of the slot is 0. A slot shorter than the word sends its first SLOT bits other values: 16
0x03 TXA transmitter: address of the first sample, 0x20 to 0xFC below 0x20: 0x20; above 0xFC: 0xFC
0x04 TXN transmitter: frames in the buffer (4 bytes each) 0: 32 (the 128-byte frame buffer); more than fit below 0x100: as many as fit
0x05 SM3 transmitter writes it: SLOT - 3 (its bit counter)
0x08 RXW receiver word length, 8 or 16: 8 keeps the first 8 bits of each word and stores 0 as the low byte other values: 16
0x09 RXA receiver: first byte of the ring, 0x20 to 0xFC below 0x20 or above 0xFC: 0xA0
0x0A RXE receiver: end of the ring (exclusive), 0 meaning 0x100; the receiver writes back the end it uses: the length RXE - RXA is rounded down to whole frames (a multiple of 4 bytes) and is at most 0x100 - RXA 0, or a length that rounds to 0: up to 0x100
0x0B RPTR receiver writes it: address where the next word goes
0x0C RCNT receiver writes it: words stored, mod 256

Samples, in both the transmitter buffer and the receiver ring, are 16-bit two's complement, high byte first, a frame being the left word then the right word: frame k of the buffer is at TXA + 4k (left high, left low, right high, right low). The default buffer is 0x20 to 0x9F, which is the frame buffer: the host writes it with ETH_W (frame byte 2j is the high byte of word j, so load/store address 0x20 + i is frame byte i), at any time, also while it plays. The rest (0xA0 up) is data memory, written with DATA_W. The default ring is 0xA0 to 0xFF (24 frames), read with DATA_R.

The transmitter plays the buffer from TXA, frame after frame, and starts again at TXA after TXN frames. It starts with one SCK period of a right word (WS high, SD 0), so the first left word is preceded by a WS change, as a receiver needs.

The receiver waits for a rising edge with WS high and then one with WS low; the next bit is the MSB of a left word. From then on it stores every word at RPTR, so left words are at even word places of the ring (from RXA, also after a wrap): a word is written during bits 1 and 2 of the word after it, and RPTR and RCNT are written at bit 3 (RPTR three ticks before RCNT). The host finds the newest word just below RPTR. RCNT counts stored words, so a host that reads less often than the ring fills (24 frames by default, 1.5 ms at 15.625 kHz) can see that it lost words. Nothing goes through the host FIFO.

How it works

Transmitter. Each SCK period is two halves on a twait grid of HALF ticks. In the falling half SCK goes low one tick after the twait and SD (shout, MSB first) one tick later; in the rising half SCK goes high one tick after the twait. A 16-bit word is two bytes: after the first byte the second one is moved in, after the second zeros, so a slot longer than the word carries zeros after the LSB, and a slot shorter than the word ends early. The last bit of a slot has its own falling half, which also changes WS (shout of the pattern 0x55, so WS alternates) one tick after SD; the halves after it load the next word, step and wrap the buffer pointer and reload the counters. No half period holds more than four instructions after its twait, so HALF 5 keeps the grid exact; with a lower minimum assembled in (HMIN) HALF 4 and 3 set the late flag and stretch some halves (Timing).

Receiver. Every bit is waitp SCK low, waitp SCK high, shin SD, shin WS. A word's bits go into two registers, bits 1 to 8 and 9 to 16; bits after 16 (or after 8 with RXW 8, through a mask) are not kept. When WS has changed at a rising edge, that bit was the word's last; if the word had fewer than 16 bits, it is shifted left with zeros. The word is copied and the next word starts at the following rising edge with its MSB. The copy is stored during the next word's bits 1 and 2 (high byte, low byte, pointer step and wrap), and RPTR and RCNT are written in bit 3, so after any rising edge the receiver runs at most five instructions before it waits for SCK again (the zero fill at the end of a word that ends inside a byte takes two ticks per missing bit more). The left and the right word are two copies of the same code, one waiting for WS high and one for WS low, so the WS test is one instruction.

test/test_i2s.py (cocotb). The models are written from UM11732 (see Source; cited in the module docstring), not from the firmware:

  • a bus reader that records every edge of SCK, WS and SD and decodes them as a UM11732 receiver: SD and WS taken at each rising edge of SCK, a word ends at the rising edge where WS has changed and the next word starts with its MSB at the following one, words before the first WS change dropped; at every rising edge it checks Table 3 (SCK HIGH and LOW at least 0.35 T, SD and WS changing after the rising edge and no later than 0.8 T, set-up before the next rising edge at least 0.2 T) and, at 2.5 MHz, Table 1;

Limits

  • Receiver hold: SD must stay unchanged 80 ns and WS 120 ns after the rising edge of SCK at the pin (Table 3 asks 0 of the transmitter and of the receiver). A transmitter that changes SD and WS on the falling edge meets this when SCK HIGH is more than 120 ns, which every SCK the receiver keeps up with (400 ns and longer, symmetric) has; a transmitter that changes them 100 ns after the rising edge (Table 1's smallest hold at 2.5 MHz) fails on WS. Taking WS before SD would swap the two numbers; both come from the two synchronizer flip-flops plus one and two ticks of shin after the waitp.
  • Receiver speed: SCK period at least 400 ns (2.5 MHz) for slots of 8, 16 and 32 bits (measured; slots of 17 to 31 bits take the code path of the 32-bit slot but were not run at speed); Table 2 asks 360 ns. Slots of 4 to 7 and 9 to 15 bits need 2 more ticks per missing bit at the word end (the zero fill): measured 720 ns for 12- and 4-bit slots and 960 ns for 9-bit slots, which have the most bits to fill (7). 64 SCK per frame at 44.1 or 48 kHz (354 and 326 ns) is shorter than the tested 360 ns, which failed.
  • Words of 1 to 3 bits are not received, although 3.1 would make them words with the missing bits 0: a left word that short is dropped together with the right word after it; a right word that short is dropped together with its left word and the next frame. After either, the receiver waits for a WS change from high to low and goes on with the next left word (test_i2s_rx_ring_and_resync). The ring keeps whole frames, left first.
  • Word lengths 8 and 16 only, on both sides (the transmitter's samples are 16-bit; a 24-bit word cannot be sent, a 24-bit word is received as its first 16 bits).
  • The transmitter makes only fs = 6.25 MHz / (SLOT x HALF): 8, 16, 32, 44.1 and 48 kHz are off by 0.032 to 0.893 % with the best slot length and by 0.35 to 1.73 % with 32 SCK per frame; 64 SCK per frame goes up to 39.06 kHz. SCK is at most 2.5 MHz. i2s_frac.pe (below) makes 32, 44.1 and 48 kHz exactly with 32 SCK per frame.
  • The transmitter plays at most 224 bytes (56 frames) from the load/store space 0x20 to 0xFF; the host must update it while it plays to send more (the frame buffer with ETH_W, which works while the harts run; the data memory only while both harts are stopped). The default buffer is the frame buffer, which the capture and the Ethernet receiver also write: a capture or a received frame overwrites the samples. The receiver's ring must not overlap the transmitter's buffer; the firmware does not check it.
  • The receiver keeps no count of lost words: if the host reads the ring more slowly than it fills, RCNT shows how many words were written, not which were overwritten.
  • No master clock (MCLK) output and no target-mode transmitter or controller-mode receiver.
  • Not tested: a real codec or microphone, gate-level simulation. On the board only i2s_frac.pe's transmitter ran (decoded by sigrok from an ILA capture, section Exact rates); i2s.pe and the receiver were not.

Exact rates with the tick generator (firmware/i2s_frac.pe, int6)

i2s_frac.pe is i2s.pe with the transmitter's grid on the hart's fractional tick generator (docs/tickgen.md): the SCK half period is N + k/M clocks from data memory 0x10 to 0x15 (N, k, M high byte first, from tools/tickgen_model.best_nkm of 4 x SLOT x fs), and its nine waits are twait.f. HALF is still clamped and written back but sets nothing; the receiver is the same. 305 of 512 words (hart 1 at word 106).

test_i2s_frac runs the transmitter alone with 16-bit words in 16-bit slots (32 SCK per frame), 80 frames per rate, and decodes with the bus reader of the tests above [measured, RTL, Icarus 11]:

The whole module with this test: TESTS=12 PASS=12 FAIL=0 (2026-11-18, RTL of int6 8574ae4, Icarus Verilog 11.0, cocotb 2.0.1). The mean rate is exact by construction (any M consecutive periods take M N + k clocks); the fit is off by less than a ppm only because 80 frames of whole-clock edge times are a short span. Each SCK half is 16 to 18 clocks at 48 kHz in periods of 32 to 34 (HIGH and LOW 0.47 to 0.56 T, Table 3 asks at least 0.35 T), SD changes 2 clocks and WS 4 clocks after the falling edge as in i2s.pe, and the late flag stays clear. 64 SCK per frame at 44.1 or 48 kHz would need halves of 8.9 and 8.1 clocks, under the 10 clocks the transmitter loop needs (four instructions per half).

On the board (by the board session; logs and the comparison in fpga/zcu104_demo/logs/i2s of the L4d worktree, commit bb2b929, not on a branch of this tree yet): the ZCU104 build of the full configuration with MBIST (bit 03fd016a, PADS loop) ran i2s_frac.pe for 30 s at each rate; an ILA took 16,384 samples of SCK, WS and SD at 50 MHz (327 us) and sigrok-cli 0.7.2 decoded them with its i2s decoder. Leaving out the first and last word cut by the window, the 28, 27 and 19 words at 48, 44.1 and 32 kHz equal the written frames in loop order, left then right, and the late flag stayed 0. The mean of the WS falls was 47,996.9, 44,097.7 and 32,001.1 Hz (-64, -52, +36 ppm); over 9 to 15 frames on a 20 ns grid that is within the resolution of the capture, about +-65 ppm [measured, board].
