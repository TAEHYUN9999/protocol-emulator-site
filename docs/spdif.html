What the bits of the channel status mean (consumer mode, sample rate code, copy bits) is in IEC 60958-3, which was not available; the firmware sends the 24 bytes the host writes.

Sample rate

A frame is 128 cells (64 per subframe, two cells per slot). A cell of CELL ticks gives a sample rate of 1 / (128 x CELL x 40 ns):

CELL Cell Sample rate Nearest standard rate
4 160 ns 48,828.1 Hz 48 kHz, +1.73 % (not possible, see below)
5 (shortest) 200 ns 39,062.5 Hz none
6 (default) 240 ns 32,552.1 Hz 32 kHz, +1.73 %
7 280 ns 27,901.8 Hz none
8 320 ns 24,414.1 Hz 24 kHz, +1.73 %
9 360 ns 21,701.4 Hz 22.05 kHz, -1.58 %

The standard rates are not a whole number of ticks: 32 kHz needs a cell of 6.10 ticks, 44.1 kHz 4.43 ticks and 48 kHz 4.07 ticks. The output is exact on its own grid but 1.7 % fast against 32 kHz; a receiver that locks to the incoming clock (most do) plays it, slightly faster, and a receiver that checks the rate against the channel status code or a range may refuse it.

The shortest cell was measured (test_spdif_fastest_cell, the minimum lowered for the run): with cells of 6 and 5 ticks 210 frames, a block start among them, decoded right with both late flags clear; with 4 ticks the stream still decoded right over those frames but hart 1's late flag was set, so its longest byte step needs more than the 31 instructions a 32-tick byte period leaves, and it would fall behind over a longer run. The setting is therefore 5 to 31, and 48 kHz-like rates are out of reach.

Pins

Pin Use
uio[0] (P0) S/PDIF out, push-pull, 3.3 V. For coaxial S/PDIF (0.5 V into 75 ohm) a resistor divider and a capacitor, or for TOSLINK the input of an optical transmitter module.

Settings and host interface

Data memory, written by the host with DATA_W while both harts are stopped (the samples with ETH_W), read with DATA_R at any time. Hart 1 checks the settings and writes back what it uses.

Address Name Meaning Out of range
0x00 CELL ticks per cell, 5 to 31 0: 6; below 5: 5; above 31: 31
0x01 NFR frames in the sample loop, 1 to 32 0 or more than 32: 32
0x02 VAL bit 0 is the V bit of every subframe
0x03 STARTF hart 1 adds 1 when it starts; hart 0 then waits 16 byte periods and starts
0x04 FRM frame number in the block, 0 to 191
0x05 SPTR address of the next sample pair
0x06, 0x07 VUCN, PVC V and C of the current frame
0x08 BLOCKS counts blocks (mod 256)
0x20..0x9F samples: frame i at 0x20 + 4i, channel 1 high, low, channel 2 high, low (frame buffer, host ETH_W)
0xA0..0xB7 channel status, 24 bytes
0xD0..0xDF hart 1: cells of each nibble
0xE0..0xFF ring of cell bytes between the harts

The samples are sent in a loop of NFR frames; a host that wants a longer signal changes them while they are not being read (the loop position is SPTR).

How it works

Hart 1 builds a table of the eight cells of every 4-slot nibble, starting from a cell of 0 (a change at each slot start, another in the middle of a 1). A subframe is eight bytes of cells: the preamble constant, two bytes of zero slots (slots 4..11), four nibbles of the sample, and the nibble of V, U, C and P. Each nibble is taken from the table and inverted when the line ends high before it; the last cell gives the polarity for the next nibble. That polarity also gives the parity: after slots 4..27 the line is at 0 if they hold an even number of ones, so P is that polarity bit xor V xor C, and with P right the line is at 0 again before the next preamble, which is why the preambles can be constants.

Hart 1 stores one byte per byte period (8 x CELL ticks, twait) into a 32-byte ring; hart 0 starts 16 byte periods later and sends one cell per CELL ticks with shout (bit 7 first). Its loop is unrolled over the eight cells of a byte and loads the next byte while sending the first two cells. Both harts keep exact periods, so the distance between writer and reader stays at 16 bytes.

Tests

test/test_spdif.py (cocotb). The decoder is written from [DIX] (module docstring), not from the firmware, and takes only the recorded edges of P0: the unit interval is the shortest interval between edges, every interval must be 1, 2 or 3 of them in whole clocks, the first preamble is found by its pattern and then one is required every 64 cells; at every slot it checks the change at the boundary and reads the bit from the middle change; per subframe it checks even parity of slots 4..31; per frame the X or Z then Y order, Z every 192 frames, and the frame length of 128 cells; it rebuilds the channel status block from the C bits. Only the host interface (addresses, sample layout, channel status byte order) is taken from this document. Run from a private directory:

T=<repo>/test; R=$T/sim_build/<name>; mkdir -p $R; cd $R
PYTHONPATH=$T make -B -f $T/Makefile PWD=$T SIM_BUILD=$R/build COCOTB_TEST_MODULES=test_spdif

One run of the whole module on 2026-11-11 (with CELL at least 5): TESTS=3 PASS=3 FAIL=0, 53.0 ms of simulated time, 236 s of wall time on the loaded server.

Limits

  • Sample rates are whole-tick cells, 1.7 % off the standard rates (see Sample rate); spdif_frac.pe makes 32, 44.1 and 48 kHz exactly.
  • 16-bit samples only (slots 4..11 are 0), U bits are 0, the channel status is the same for both channels, and the samples loop over at most 32 frames (1 ms at 32.5 kHz) unless the host rewrites them while they play.
  • The channel status is sent as written; the firmware does not check that it describes the stream (consumer mode, rate code), because IEC 60958-3 was not available.
  • Both harts are used; nothing else can run beside it.
  • spdif.pe: simulation only. spdif_frac.pe: also the FPGA build, decoded by sigrok from a 327 us ILA capture per rate; no S/PDIF receiver was attached to either.

Exact rates with the tick generator (firmware/spdif_frac.pe, int6)

spdif_frac.pe is spdif.pe with the hart timer replaced by the fractional tick generators (docs/tickgen.md). The host writes the cell (hart 0) and the byte of 8 cells (hart 1) as N + k/M clocks at data memory 0x09 to 0x0E and 0x0F to 0x14 (N, k, M high byte first, from tools/tickgen_model.best_nkm of 128 fs and 16 fs); every wait is twait.f. Both means are exact, so hart 1 stays 16 bytes ahead of hart 0 as before. CELL (0x00) is no longer used.

test_spdif_frac decodes P0 with the decoder of the tests above, renumbered in cells: every interval between edges must be 1, 2 or 3 cells within 2 clocks [measured, RTL, Icarus 11]:

fs Cell (clocks) Run Decoded Fit of every edge Edges vs ideal grid Late flags
32 kHz 12 + 53/256 = 12.207 13 ms 412 frames, 2 block starts 192 apart, both channel status blocks, samples, parity, V, U 32,000.000 Hz (+0.00 ppm) 2 clocks (40 ns, 0.164 UI) peak to peak clear
44.1 kHz 8 + 1513/1764 = 8.858 6 ms 260 frames, 1 block start, samples, parity, V, U 44,100.000 Hz (-0.01 ppm) 2 clocks (0.226 UI) clear
48 kHz 8 + 53/384 = 8.138 6 ms 284 frames, 1 block start, samples, parity, V, U 48,000.000 Hz (-0.00 ppm) 2 clocks (0.245 UI) clear

The whole module with this test: TESTS=4 PASS=4 FAIL=0, 895 s (2026-11-18, RTL of int6 8574ae4, Icarus Verilog 11.0, cocotb 2.0.1; the test then judged only 32 kHz, the run after it judges all three: 1 of 1, 277 s). The edges lie within 2 clocks peak to peak of the ideal line, as docs/tickgen.md gives for any firmware that writes a pin after twait.f. 48 kHz keeps up although spdif.pe with a cell of 4 ticks (48,828 Hz) made hart 1 late (Sample rate above): a byte of 8 cells is 32.55 ticks at 48 kHz and 32 at a cell of 4, so hart 1's longest byte step needs between 32 and 33 ticks, and the margin at 48 kHz is under one tick. The jitter limits of IEC 60958 and AES3 were not available (Source), so the 2 clocks are reported, not judged; a receiver that recovers its clock from the stream sees them as jitter at the generator's pattern (M = 256, 1764 and 384 cells).

On the board (by the board session; logs and the comparison in fpga/zcu104_demo/logs/spdif of the L4d worktree, commit 6aee8cd, not on a branch of this tree yet): the ZCU104 build of the full configuration with MBIST (bit 03fd016a, P0 through the pad model of the PADS loop) ran spdif_frac.pe at 48, 44.1 and 32 kHz; an ILA took 16,384 samples of P0 at 50 MHz (327 us) and sigrok-cli 0.7.2 decoded them with its spdif decoder. Of the 29, 28 and 19 complete subframes, every Audio field held the written sample in its upper 16 bits in loop order with the lower 8 bits 0, M and W alternated (the 44.1 kHz capture holds one block start, B in the place of M), and both late flags were 0. sigrok's "Sample" annotation is the bit string in transmit order, so "Audio" was compared [measured, board].
