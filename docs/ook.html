File Role Words
firmware/ook.pe The data line of an OOK (on-off keying) transmitter or receiver module, 1 = carrier on. Hart 0 sends, hart 1 receives; each picks the code from the data memory. Remote code: the 1:3 / 3:1 pulse code of EV1527 and PT2262 encoders, sync 1:31, 24 pulse bits most significant first, repeated; the receiver measures every high and low, finds the sync by its ratio (no fixed T), checks the 1:3 / 3:1 ratios and reports a code when two words in a row are the same, once per transmission. Manchester: IEEE 802.3 direction (a 1 rises in the middle of its cell), preamble of 16 ones, a 0, length, bytes least significant bit first, 8-bit sum; the receiver locks on the preamble and re-times every bit on its middle edge. 285 of 512 (hart 1 from word 103)

No RTL change: tset/tsetr/twait grids for sending, waitp.to time-outs counted in units for the pulse widths, the host FIFO. Checked in simulation only, not on a board.

Sources

Source Used for
rc-switch library (github.com/sui77/rc-switch, RCSwitch.cpp), protocol 1 pulse length 350 us; sync 1 high + 31 low, 0 = 1 high + 3 low, 1 = 3 high + 1 low (in pulse lengths); bits sent most significant first, the sync after each word, the word repeated; PT2262 tri-state digits as two bits (0 = 00, F = 01, 1 = 11). This is an open-source implementation of the EV1527/PT2262 format, a secondary source: the EV1527 data sheet could not be fetched and the PT2262 data sheet found (Princeton Technology, short form) has no waveform figures
IEEE 802.3 Clause 7.3.1.1 as quoted in docs/architecture.md Manchester direction: first half of a cell the complement of the bit, second half the bit
this firmware the Manchester frame (16 ones, 0, length, bytes LSB first, sum). Oregon Scientific v2.1/v3 sensors use Manchester OOK at 1024 bit/s with their own nibble format, which is not modelled; the frame here is a general one

Timing

Remote code: one pulse length T is TH:TL ticks (8,750: 350 us). Each edge of the sender is one tick after a period boundary: a 0 is exactly T high and 3 T low, a 1 3 T high and T low, the sync T high and 31 T low. The receiver counts highs and lows in units of U ticks (625: 25 us); a low of 8 times the high before it is a sync (recognized after 8 T, not at its end, so a word is reported without waiting for the next transmission); a bit needs the longer part at least twice the shorter. A low of 512 units (12.8 ms) ends a transmission, so the same code sent again later is reported again.

Manchester: half a bit MHH:MHL ticks (12,207: 1024 bit/s); every edge, including the last one of a frame, comes two ticks after a half-cell boundary. The receiver waits three quarters of a bit (MQH:MQL) after each middle edge, reads the first half of the next cell (its complement) and then needs the middle edge within half a bit.

Tests

test/test_ook.py, run from a private directory:

T=<repo>/test; R=$T/sim_build/<name>; mkdir -p $R; cd $R
PYTHONPATH=$T make -B -f $T/Makefile PWD=$T SIM_BUILD=$R/build COCOTB_TEST_MODULES=test_ook

The models are written from the sources above, not from the firmware: the pulse train of a word with its sync, tri-state digits, Manchester cells and frames, a decoder of a recorded line that takes every high and low as exactly 1, 3 or 31 T, and senders with jitter on every pulse. Most tests run at 1/10 of the times (T = 35 us, 10,240 bit/s); two run at the default times.

Limits

  • Remote code: 24 pulse bits only (no 12-bit HT12E or other protocols of rc-switch), no rolling codes; the sync must be at least 8 times its high part.
  • Manchester: the frame format is this firmware's own; no Oregon Scientific nibble decoding, no checksum other than the 8-bit sum.
  • The RF part (433.92 MHz carrier, ASK modulation, receiver squelch) is outside the chip.
  • Simulation only: no board, no gate-level run.
