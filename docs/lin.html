File Role Words
firmware/lin.pe LIN 2.2A master on hart 0 and slave on hart 1, on one open-drain wire or on the TXD and RXD pins of a LIN transceiver. The master sends the header and sends or receives the response for the host, sends wake-up signals and reports those it sees. The slave answers or takes the frames in a table in the data memory, measures the bit time on every sync byte (auto-baud), catches a break that starts inside a byte, goes to bus sleep on the go to sleep command or after bus inactivity, and wakes up on a wake-up signal or wakes the bus itself. 391 of 512 (hart 1 starts at word 163)

This protocol was not part of the design when the RTL was written. It uses only existing instructions (low/rel, shout.lsb.od, shin.lsb, tset/tsetr/twait, waitp.to.frz with tgeth/tgetl, the CRC unit, ld/st, gpi, host FIFO); the RTL is unchanged. Checked in simulation only, not on a board.

Pins

Two assembler constants choose the pins: TXD, which the firmware drives, and RXD, which it reads. Every edge, bit sample and readback comes from RXD.

Variant Constants Pins
One wire (default) TXD = RXD = 0 uio[0] (P0) is the LIN bus, open drain: driven low (dominant) or released (recessive). Needs a pull-up.
Transceiver TXD = 0, RXD = 1 (assemble(text, {"RXD": 1})) P0 drives the transceiver's TXD input, P1 reads its RXD output.

Both harts use the same pins. Each hart drives them through its own output and enable registers and a pin is the wired AND of the two (docs/dual_role.md), so the master hart and the slave hart are two nodes on one bus: a dominant bit of either wins. LIN has one sender at a time anyway (2.3.3.1: one publisher per frame; the master alone sends the header), and low, rel and shout.od change only the one bit of the pin they name. On the RTL of branch mem512 (revisions) the harts shared one pin_out/pin_oe register pair, so two harts sending at once got the later write instead of the dominant level; test_lin_two_publishers shows the difference (Tests, Limits). The test bus outside the chip is a real wired-AND, so a model node can collide with the chip.

Transceiver

A LIN transceiver turns the logic-level TXD into the bus and the bus into RXD; one pin cannot do both (once another node pulls the bus dominant, RXD would pull the pin low and hold TXD dominant). The TXD/RXD variant drives TXD open drain like the one-wire bus, so TXD needs a pull-up: the TJA1021 has only a weak pull-down on TXD (RPD(TXD) 140 kOhm minimum, Table 7) and its section 7.8 mentions an external pull-up on TXD. Its RXD is open drain (Table 3) and needs a pull-up too. The firmware does not drive SLP_N: the transceiver stays in Normal mode, and the firmware's bus sleep is the protocol state of 2.6, not the transceiver's Sleep mode.

The test model (XcvrBus in test/test_lin.py) is written from the TJA1021 data sheet (NXP, Rev. 9, 10 February 2023):

The model takes the slowest edge the data sheet allows, and delays that keep the duty cycles inside Table 8. Model nodes drive and read the modelled bus directly (P2 shows it read at 0.5 VBAT), and the frame reader judges the frames there; the chip sees the bus only through the model's receiver.

The firmware needs no change of timing for this: the readback samples RXD at the end of each bit, 52.08 us after the TXD edge, and the loop delay is 27.5 us at most; the slave measures the bit time between falling edges of RXD, which are all delayed alike; the response space on the bus grows by the loop delay (slave publishing: 1.032 bits, against 0.50 on one wire).

Every byte field the chip sends is on a twait grid of one bit: each of its edges comes two ticks after a period boundary, so the start bit and every data bit are exactly one bit time, to the clock (test_lin_slave and test_lin_master check every edge of the byte fields the chip sends). txbyte is called inside the period before the start bit and waits for its end; it returns inside the stop bit, and the master ends that stop bit with a twait before it reports a status or listens for a response. Until 2026-11-11 the start bit began when txbyte was called, 3 to 17 ticks into the running period depending on the caller, so it was 2 to 16 ticks short of a bit, and data bit 0 was 2 ticks long. Each data bit is read back at the end of its bit time, where the bus has settled even with slow LIN edges; a dominant pulse that ends before the end of a recessive bit is therefore not seen as a bit error.

The long waits count periods of 65538 ticks (the timer is 16 bits: 2.6 ms at most) in two nested loops, so each is set by two assembler constants of at most 255. The simulations run the same code with 2 to 5 periods in place of the defaults (IDLH, IDLL, W2H, W2L, W3H, W3L given to the assembler); the measured durations are the period count plus the few dozen ticks of the instructions around the waits, and the defaults are that period times their count. A 4 s simulation would take Icarus about 11 hours at the speed of these runs.

Master (hart 0)

Host to chip (FIFO): c, n, then the n data bytes when the master publishes.

Byte Meaning
c bits 5..0 Frame identifier, 0 to 63
c bit 6 1: the master publishes the response, 0: a slave does
c bit 7 1: classic checksum (LIN 1.x slaves, and identifiers 0x3C and 0x3D, 2.3.1.5), 0: enhanced
n Data bytes, 1 to 8. n = 0 with c = 0: send a wake-up signal. Any other value: the command is ignored and nothing is sent.

The data bytes are taken into the data memory before the break, so the line timing does not depend on the host. The go to sleep command is an ordinary frame: c = 0xFC (0x3C, the master publishes, classic), n = 8, data 0x00 and seven 0xFF. After each command the chip answers one status byte:

Status Meaning
0 OK. When a slave published, the n data bytes follow. Also the answer to the wake-up command.
1 No response: no start bit within 65535 ticks after the header
2 Response too short: the same after at least one byte of the response
3 Framing error: a stop bit was dominant
4 Checksum error
5 Bit error: a bit the master sent read back different; the master stopped after that byte
6 Not an answer: while waiting for a command the master saw a wake-up signal on the bus (dominant longer than 150 us, then recessive)

The master compares the received checksum with the inverted sum exactly. Adding the received checksum with carry and testing for 0xFF, as in the 2.8.3 example, also accepts 0xFF where 0x00 is due (sum 0xFF: 0xFF + 0xFF with the carry added is 0xFF again).

While it waits for a command, the master polls the host FIFO (rxb.nb) and the bus in turn, every 5 ticks (200 ns). Any dominant pulse longer than 150 us there is reported as status 6, also one that is not a wake-up signal, such as a response that comes after the master gave up. After status 3 or 5 another node may still be sending, so the master first waits until the bus has been recessive for 65535 ticks. Status 6 can come between a command and its status when both happen at once; it is never a frame status.

Slave (hart 1)

Data memory, written by the host with DATA_W before the run and read with DATA_R at any time:

Address Use
0x13 SNUM Number of table entries, 0 to 9
0xA0 + 10 k Entry k: byte 0 bits 5..0 frame identifier, bit 6 1 = publish (the slave sends the response), 0 = subscribe, bit 7 1 = classic checksum; byte 1 n (1 to 8); bytes 2 to 9 the data
0x0A, 0x0B STH, STL: the bit time measured on the last sync byte, ticks
0x0E SHDR: headers seen (break and a sync byte of plausible length)
0x0F SOK: frames published or received without error
0x10 SERR: frames with an error in the response: framing, checksum, readback, or a response cut short by the next break
0x14 SSLP: 1 in bus sleep, 0 operational
0x15 SWK: wake-up signals (or breaks) that woke the slave from bus sleep
0x16 SWS: wake-up signals the slave sent
0x00 to 0x09, 0x0C, 0x0D, 0x11, 0x12, 0x17 to 0x1F Used by the firmware

For a subscribed frame the bytes are collected apart and copied into the entry only when the checksum is right (2.2.4), so a bad frame leaves the old data. A PID whose parity is wrong, or whose identifier is not in the table, is ignored: no response, no counter but SHDR. Identifiers in the table must differ. Identifier 0x3C (master request frame) is received in any case, as classic with 8 bytes; when it is not in the table the bytes stay in the firmware's buffer. A slave never publishes 0x3C.

Break inside a byte

2.3.1.2 requires a slave to find a break while it waits for a byte field, and calls it desired, not required, to find one that is partially superimposed with a data byte. srx does both. A break that starts inside a byte makes the stop bit dominant. At the stop bit sample the bus has then been dominant since the end of the last recessive data bit: z + 0.5 bits for a byte whose z top bits (bit 7 down) are 0, or 9.5 bits for a 0 byte, whose start bit is dominant too. The slave waits 21 - 2z more nominal half bits (3 for a 0 byte), one waitp.to of 651 ticks each: a recessive level on the way is a framing error, otherwise 11 bits are reached and the frame is a new header, as for a break between bytes. The count needs no multiplication: z comes from shifting the byte left until a 1 drops out, two half bits off each time.

Bus sleep and wake-up

Event What the slave does
Go to sleep received: identifier 0x3C, right classic checksum, first data byte 0 counts it in SOK, then bus sleep (SSLP = 1)
No falling edge for IDLH x IDLL periods (5.0 s) while waiting for a break bus sleep

In bus sleep: a shorter dominant pulse stays in bus sleep

In bus sleep the hart polls RXD and ui[3] every 6 ticks (240 ns) and answers nothing. ui[3] is a level: the slave asks for wake-ups as long as it is high and the bus sleeps.

Auto-baud

The slave counts ticks from the falling edge of the sync byte's start bit to the falling edge of bit 7, 8 bits later (6.4.2), with the timer frozen by waitp.frz on that edge, rounds the count divided by 8, and uses it for the PID and the response of that frame, sending and receiving. A count outside 8192 to 13311 ticks is not a sync byte and the frame is ignored.

Master bit rate Result Why
-15 % to +15 % works: tested at -15, -10, -2, 0, +2, +10, +15 %
up to +18.2 % works: tested at +16, +18 % the master's 13-bit break is still at least 11 nominal bits long
above +18.2 % the break is not seen: tested at +19, +20 % 13 / 11 = 1.182: the threshold is fixed in nominal bits, as 2.3.1.1 asks
down to -21.7 % works: tested at -18, -21 %
below -21.7 % the sync byte is not accepted: tested at -22, -25 % 8 bits are 13312 ticks or more, outside the accepted window

The specification asks a synchronizing slave to cope with a clock that is up to 14 % off before synchronization (FTOL_UNSYNC, Table 6.1) and to send within 2 % of the master after it (FTOL_SYNC, Table 6.2). Here the master is off instead of the slave, which is the same ratio seen from the bus. In the tests the measured bit time was within one tick of the master's (1094 to 1648 ticks), and the slave's response bit time was 0.999 to 1.000 of the master's.

The pins, the break inside a byte and sleep with wake-up took 98 words over the 292 of the first version (TXD/RXD: 0, they are assembler constants). Putting the byte fields on the bit grid (commit c0e6599) took one more: txbyte waits for the period before its start bit (+1), the master ends the PID's stop bit before a slave response and the checksum's stop bit before its status (+2) and drops one twait from the header (-1), and the slave drops the twait at spub (-1).

pid uses the CRC unit as a parity unit. The CRC register is an affine function of the bits fed in, so some polynomial can leave a parity of chosen bits in a chosen register bit. A search over all 65536 polynomials found that polynomial 0x0F74 with start value 0xFFFF and seven LSB-first steps (crc.lsb r0,7) leave P0 in bit 6 and P1 in bit 7, the inversion of P1 coming from the start value. The PID is then the identifier OR the register's low byte AND 0xC0: 11 words and 11 ticks for both parity bits. A first draft that counted the bits in a loop took 28 words for the two. The routine sets the polynomial and start value on every call, so it works from either hart whatever the hart's CRC unit held before (LIN uses the CRC unit for nothing else); test_lin_pid_parity runs it for all 64 identifiers.

Tests

test/test_lin.py (cocotb). The models are written from the specification, not from the firmware: a frame reader that finds breaks of 11 nominal bits or more (also one that begins inside a byte), measures the bit time on the sync byte and requires every bit to be stable between 7/16 and 9/16 of its bit time; a master model that drives headers and responses at any bit rate, with options to break the frame on purpose; a slave model that does not synchronize (fixed bit rate) and answers its frames after a response space; the TJA1021 model above. They share P0 with the chip as a wired-AND, or the modelled bus behind the transceiver. Only the host interface (commands, status bytes, data memory addresses, the assembler constants for the pins and the long waits) is taken from this document. Run from a private directory:

T=<repo>/test; R=$T/sim_build/<name>; mkdir -p $R; cd $R
PYTHONPATH=$T COCOTB_TESTCASE=<test> make -B -f $T/Makefile PWD=$T SIM_BUILD=$R/build COCOTB_TEST_MODULES=test_lin

Each test is run on its own (COCOTB_TESTCASE matches the whole name; COCOTB_TEST_FILTER is a regular expression searched in the name, so test_lin_slave there would also pick the other test_lin_slave_* tests); test_lin_slave simulates 86 ms and took about 14 minutes on an idle machine.

Limits

  • Two harts sending at once: since branch adv a pin is the wired AND of the two harts' drives, so the dominant level wins as on the bus, and test_lin_two_publishers passes on the integrated RTL (Tests). On the RTL of branch mem512 the harts shared one pin_out/pin_oe register pair and the later write won; the test is skipped there.
  • A break that begins while the slave is itself sending (the master's break over a response) or inside the sync byte is not caught: the slave stops at the readback error, or drops the sync measurement, and waits for the next break, so the frame after it is missed. 2.3.1.2 asks for the case where the slave expects a byte field, which srx covers, also inside a byte.
  • The long waits (5.0 s, 199 ms, 1.6 s) were simulated with 2 to 5 periods only; the defaults are the measured period times their count. They follow the 50 MHz clock: the 5.0 s timeout stays within 4 to 10 s for a clock between 0.51 and 1.25 times 50 MHz, the 199 ms silence within 150 to 250 ms between 0.80 and 1.32 times.
  • Bus sleep is a protocol state: the hart keeps polling, nothing is powered down, and the transceiver's SLP_N, INH and WAKE_N are not used. ui[3] is a level, not an edge.
  • While it waits for a command the master reports any dominant pulse longer than 150 us, not only wake-up signals (Master).
  • Readback is sampled once per bit, at its end. Behind a transceiver the TXD to RXD delay must stay below one bit: 27.5 us in the slowest model, 52.08 us bit.
  • The transceiver is a model written from the TJA1021 data sheet. The data sheet text gives ttx_pd without its measurement point, so the model takes it as the delay to the start of the bus edge and checks the result against the duty cycles of Table 8. Not tested: a real transceiver and bus (ground shift, the transceiver's filters, other parts such as the MCP2003B), gate-level simulation, the board.
  • Not implemented: event triggered and sporadic frames, schedule tables, the diagnostic transport layer.
