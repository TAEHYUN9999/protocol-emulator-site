File Role Words
firmware/eeprom93.pe A Microchip 93LC46B (64 words of 16 bits, Microwire) seen from the bus: start bit, READ with the dummy 0 and sequential reading, EWEN/EWDS, WRITE, ERASE, ERAL, WRAL with the self-timed cycle from the CS fall, busy/ready on DO. Hart 0 decodes and drives DO only while reading, and its waits for each CLK edge of a read also end on CS low (waitp2); hart 1 shows the status at each CS rise, turns busy into ready with its own copy of the cycle timer, releases DO at each CS fall and counts CS falls. 244 of 512 (hart 1 starts at word 216)

This protocol was not part of the design when the RTL was written. The earlier version (commit 17f58c9) uses only the first instruction set and its extensions; this version uses waitp2 (a wait on two pins, docs/architecture.md), added in branch int3 for it, and needs that RTL. Checked in simulation only, not on a board.

DO has one driver at a time, and each hart releases only what it drove itself, so the program does the same on the RTL where the two harts share one pin output/enable register (the mem512 design) and on the int2 RTL, where each hart has its own pair and a pin is the wired AND of the two (docs/dual_role.md on that branch). The first version had hart 1 show busy and hart 0 switch DO to ready, and let hart 1's rel at the CS fall end hart 0's read data; on the int2 RTL hart 1's low stayed on the pin and every status check timed out ("busy too long"). On the int2 RTL (revisions) the version of 17f58c9 still missed TCZ at the end of a READ: hart 0 drove the read data and could not wait for the next CLK rise and for CS low at once, so it let go of DO only when its wait for CLK timed out (1000 to 1390 ns). With waitp2 its waits for CLK end on CS low too (ob8).

Section numbers are those of the Microchip data sheet DS20001749K, "93AA46A/B/C, 93LC46A/B/C, 93C46A/B/C 1K Microwire Compatible Serial EEPROM" (2002-2013). The x16 organisation (B device) was chosen; the timing limits are those of the 2.5 V to 4.5 V column of Table 1-2.

Timing

Every CLK wait in hart 0 while it decodes (rb, rbd) times out after TMO = 32 ticks and then looks at CS; while it sends read data (ob8) the wait itself ends on CS low (waitp2). A CS low pulse shorter than that (TCSL is only 250 ns) is caught through hart 1's counter of CS falls (CSC), which hart 0 compares at every CLK rise and at every timeout of rb: the pulse ends the instruction or the sequential read, and when the rise that follows it has DI high it is taken as the next start bit. The path from that rise to the wait for the next CLK low is 11 ticks, which is what lets the start bit and the first opcode bit be told apart at 2 MHz (one CLK period is 18.75 ticks). The status levels are written to ST as 40h (busy) and 80h (ready), so hart 1 drives DO with one shout 5 ticks after it sees CS rise. Hart 1 loads its timer with the programming time at every CS fall except those of status checks during a cycle; when a CS rise finds ST = 40h, the timer is the one started at the CS fall that began the cycle, a tick after hart 0's, and waitp.to CS, 0 ends either at the CS fall or when it runs out (then high DO).

Tests

test/test_eeprom93.py (cocotb). The model and the host are written from the data sheet (sections in the module docstring): a 93LC46B model that runs each instruction from its bits, and a Microwire host that sets DI in the middle of CLK low and reads DO just before the next rise. Every DO change and DO enable change is recorded with its time; TPD is measured from each CLK rise to a DO change, TCZ from each CS fall to DO high-Z, TSV from each status check's CS rise to DO driven. Only the host interface above is taken from this page. Run from a private directory:

T=<repo>/test; R=$T/sim_build/<name>; mkdir -p $R; cd $R
PYTHONPATH=$T COCOTB_TESTCASE=<test> make -B -f $T/Makefile PWD=$T SIM_BUILD=$R/build COCOTB_TEST_MODULES=test_eeprom93

The release at the end of a read is two ticks after hart 0 sees CS low (the waitp2 that ends and jc obx, then rel DO), about 40 ns more than hart 1's release after the status (waitp CS, 0, then rel DO). TPD did not change: the wait for the rise and the branch before shout take as many ticks as waitp.to and jz did.

Before this version, the same two tests were run with the file of 17f58c9 on the mem512 RTL (one shared pin register: 2/2 PASS, TCZ 100 to 120 ns) and on the int2 RTL (each test FAIL at TCZ, the numbers above); that version is kept in the history for those RTLs.

The CS low time between instructions in both tests is 600 ns, shorter than TMO; before the CS fall counter was added, such a pulse was missed and the next instruction was misread.

Limits

  • One organisation (x16). Programming times up to 65535 ticks (2.6 ms); the data sheet's maxima are 6 ms (TWC, TEC) and 15 ms (TWL), so the emulated cycle is always within them.
  • CLK up to 2 MHz (the 93LC46B limit at 2.5 V and up) checked; the 93XX46C's 3 MHz is not.
  • Needs waitp2 (int3 RTL or later). On an older RTL a waitp2 word is a waitp on its first pin (the bits of the second were ignored), so a read would end only at the next CLK edge; use the version of commit 17f58c9 there.
  • A CS fall in the tick or two after a CLK rise, before hart 0 has shifted out the bit, leaves DO driven until hart 0 is back in its wait (the shift, the CS fall counter check and djnz, about 6 ticks): estimated up to about 280 ns after the CS fall, not measured; a host keeps CS high at least half a clock after the last rise in practice (the tests: 500 ns at 1 MHz).
  • A status check whose CS rise comes in the few ticks after hart 1's timer ran out but before hart 0 wrote ready to ST shows busy for about 200 ns, then ready.
  • Simulation only: no board. At gate level only on a netlist of the int3 RTL synthesized with Yosys and the IHP typical library, not hardened: 2/2 with the same TPD, TCZ and TSV as at RTL.
