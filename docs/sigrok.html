Third-party decode with sigrok

The cocotb models (test/test_*.py) and the board's ILA decoder (fpga/board/decode_ila.py) were written on this project, by the same author as the firmware. A misreading of a bus can therefore be shared by the firmware and the check that passes it. This page hands the same pin waveforms to the protocol decoders of sigrok (libsigrokdecode), which nobody on this project wrote, and compares what they decode with what our models judged, byte by byte.

tools/sigrok_judge.py does all of it; this page gives the method, the versions and the results.

The packages come from the Ubuntu archive only and are unpacked without root:

mkdir deb && cd deb
apt-get download sigrok-cli libsigrok4 libsigrokdecode4 libzip4 libserialport0 libhidapi-hidraw0
for d in *.deb; do dpkg -x "$d" ../sigrok_root; done; cd ..
export LD_LIBRARY_PATH=$PWD/sigrok_root/usr/lib/x86_64-linux-gnu
export SIGROKDECODE_DIR=$PWD/sigrok_root/usr/share/libsigrokdecode/decoders
export SIGROK_CLI=$PWD/sigrok_root/usr/bin/sigrok-cli
$SIGROK_CLI --version     # sigrok-cli 0.7.2, libsigrok 0.5.2, libsigrokdecode 0.5.3 (Python 3.10)

The other libraries libsigrok needs (glib, libusb, libftdi, libieee1284, libbluetooth, Python 3.10) were already on the machine.

Method

Waveforms from the simulation. Each case reruns one cocotb test (make -B EDGE_LOG=<file> FST=-none). test/edge_log.v writes a line at every falling clock edge at which a protocol pin or uo[2..7] changed: the chip's uio_oe and uio_out, and the test's own drive (ext_oe, ext_out). The tool turns it into the line level the bench itself forms (test/tb.v: the chip's level where it drives, the test's where only the test drives, else 1 for the pull-up) and writes one 1-bit VCD wire per pin, p0 to p7, with a 10 ns time unit (sigrok derives a 100 MHz sample rate from it). A change is placed at its clock (20 ns). Segments between soft resets are cut where a test runs several settings in a row (the nine UART formats).

The edge log rounds a line the test changes inside a clock to the clock edge. Only MDIO needs better: its PHY models answer 10 ns after the MDC rise, and on the rounded waveform sigrok reads every read-data bit one MDC period late. That case takes the bench's FST dump instead (FST=-fst, read through GTKWave's fst2vcd, only tb.p0 to tb.p7, 1 ns steps); the table shows both.

Waveforms from the boards. The ILA captures of the self test (evidence/board_1110e_selftest/ila/ for bit 1110e, and the same three files of the int1 bit of 2026-11-11: zcu_uart.csv, zcu_spi.csv, zcu_i2c.csv; 16,384 samples of pin_in, the level the chip sees, at the 50 MHz chip clock) are written to a VCD the same way. They are compared with fpga/board/decode_ila.py (same author) and with the frames that fpga/gen_script.py defines.

What is compared. sigrok's decode, byte by byte (or frame by frame where the decoder gives frames), against what our model judged in the same run: the values the cocotb test printed from its model where it prints them (UART, CAN, PS/2, Modbus, NEC, SWD, W25Q, 1-Wire), otherwise the values the test asserts its model saw, rebuilt from the test module's own constants, seeds and generators (it is imported, not copied). A case passes when the bytes are equal, sigrok reports no error on lines that should be clean, and the cocotb test itself passed in that run.

Controls. A decoder that shows nothing wrong on a broken line is no judge. The tool breaks waveforms that decode cleanly (a stop bit held low, one data bit inverted, one USB bit sent as K instead of J) and checks that sigrok reports the error on the broken copy and not on the clean one.

Run everything (about 1 hour of simulation, one test at a time in test/):

python3 tools/sigrok_judge.py all --work $TMPDIR/sigrok_work --rerun \
    --ila evidence/board_1110e_selftest/ila <folder of the int1 board capture>

sim <case> runs single cases, board <ila dir> only the board captures, vcd-edges and vcd-ila only convert. Without --rerun a case reuses the edge log already in the work folder.

What the decoders could not judge

A third-party decoder has its own reading of the bus, and libsigrokdecode 0.5.3 is not the newest release. Where it disagreed with our model, the cause was traced to its source (/usr/share/libsigrokdecode/decoders/<name>/pd.py in the package) before anything else was believed. None of the disagreements below is a fault of the chip; each is listed with what the tool does about it.

Decoder What it does Effect here What the tool does
ps2 Emits a frame only at the twelfth falling CLK edge and drops that edge (handle_bits: the frame is decoded after the 12th bit is appended, then the bit list is cleared) In back-to-back frames it eats the next frame's start bit; from the second key on the bytes are wrong (12 of 15 decoded, 1 right) Adds one 2 us CLK pulse 10 us after each frame (inside the idle time, DATA high), then all 15 bytes agree. Both decodes are shown
rgb_led_ws281x Takes a bit as 1 when its high time is more than half the bit (duty / period > 0.5, comment "ideal T0H 33 %, T1H 66 %"); only the last bit of a frame uses a high time of at least 625 ns ws2812.pe sends a 1 as 640 ns high and 640 ns low, exactly 50 %, inside the WS2812B-V5 windows (T1H and T1L 580 ns to 1 us); the decoder reads every 1 but the last bit of a frame as 0 Shown as information only; no workaround, since the decoder's rule is not the datasheet's
dmx512 Takes the first run of either level longer than 88 us as the break, looks for a break again only after slot 512, and reads each bit at the end of its 4 us The line idles high before the first break, the controller sends 8 slots, and the clock-exact edges fall on the decoder's sampling points, so the slot values are wrong Uses dmx512 only for the break and the mark after break, each packet in its own window; reads the slots (UART frames, 250 kbit/s, 8N2) with the uart decoder
ir_rc5 Ends a frame at the middle of its 14th bit and takes the next edge of either kind as the start of a new frame When the last bit is a 1 that edge is the end of the frame's own last burst; test_rc5_tx sends its second frame 1 ms after the first (two key presses back to back), and the decoder loses it (2 of 3 frames) Decodes each frame in its own window, from 0.4 ms before its first edge to 24.3 ms after it; then 3 of 3. Both decodes are shown
can is_valid_crc() returns True ("TODO"); stuffing errors are not reported A frame with one data bit inverted decodes without an error Frames are compared field by field (identifier, DLC, data, CRC field, ACK) with the model's decode; the broken-frame row is kept as information
lin Stays in its frame state after a header with no response Decodes the frames up to and including the header-only 0x20, then nothing (the last frame, with its checksum wrong on purpose, is not decoded); flags 0x05, which the test's slave table sends with the classic checksum, as "Checksum invalid" under LIN 2 rules (correct for LIN 2) Compares the bytes on the wire with the uart decoder; the lin verdicts are shown as information
modbus Separates frames by a gap (28 bit times by default) and has no t1.5 rule The request with a 1.7-character gap inside it, which the chip correctly drops (MODBUS over Serial Line 2.5.1.1), decodes as a good frame with a correct CRC Compares the bytes on both lines; the frame verdicts are shown as information
cec Emits the ACK of the last block only after more idle time than the test leaves (it ends 0.9 ms after that ACK bit) Without padding, the TV's polling message decodes without its ACK Holds the released, idle-high line 5 ms past the end of the simulation
mdio Samples MDIO at the MDC rising edge Correct; but the PHY models change MDIO 10 ns after the edge, which the clock-rounded edge log moves onto the edge, so read data comes out one bit late Reads the bench's FST dump with exact times for this case

What this does not show

  • The waveforms are the RTL simulation's and, for UART, SPI and I2C only, the FPGA board's. sigrok reads the same pins a second time; it does not replace a real external device (docs/verification.md, section 11).
  • Except for MDIO, a line the test drives is placed at the clock edge it changed in; timing finer than 20 ns is not judged here (the cocotb tests judge it).
  • HDLC, SENT, DShot, SMBus, the TMP102 register map and the Ethernet frames have no matching decoder in libsigrokdecode 0.5.3 or were not tried.
  • The decoder version is the one Ubuntu 22.04 ships (libsigrokdecode 0.5.3). Some of the limits above may be fixed in later releases; no decoder was patched here.
