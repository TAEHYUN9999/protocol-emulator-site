A protocol rule such as "tLOW is at least 4.7 us" was written down several times in this repository, by the same author, in different forms: in the on-chip witness (firmware/witness_i2c.pe, its defaults in ticks) and in the cocotb models (the bus monitor and SPEC of test/test_i2c.py, the reference checker of test/test_witness.py). A wrong number or a misread clause could hide in any of them, and a check written twice by one person tends to agree with itself.

This page describes the other way round: the rules of one protocol at one speed are written once, in a table that cites the clause and table of the source for each rule, and three judges are generated from it:

Judge Generated file Runs on Reads
(a) witness firmware/gen/witness_<table>.pe the chip, hart 1, while it runs the protocol pins: I2C and SPI every 4 ticks (160 ns), UART at its sample points, 1-Wire at every edge (waitp.frz)
(b) cocotb judge test/judges/<table>.py the cocotb test the recorded pin changes, exact times
(c) sigrok judge tools/judges/sigrok_<table>.py the host, after the run a VCD of the pins and the output of sigrok's protocol decoder

The three are then given the same waveforms, hundreds of them, normal and broken, and must agree violation by violation. Where they did not, the waveform was examined and the judge that was wrong was fixed; that record is in Disagreements found and fixed.

Related work, for the idea and not for code: the Corelis CAS-1000 compares measured times with "maximum and minimum values loaded from a specification file"; MBAC (Boulé and Zilic) generates hardware assertion checkers from PSL/SVA; R2U2 runs temporal-logic monitors on an FPGA. Here the checker is generated as firmware for a CPU whose instructions are pin operations, so no RTL change was needed. The results on this page ran on the 512-word RTL (the src/ of commit 88be6aa, the same as that of 7c40aac); on this tree, with the src/ of 422ec10, test_spec passes 3 of 3 (verification.md section 1). The 1-Wire table was added later and ran on the RTL of the tree it was added to (src/*.v of 8c98be2), where all four tests of test_spec passed, one at a time (1-Wire). tools/pegen.py is the opposite direction: a table of line levels that generates a transmitter.

Table format

spec/*.json, one file per protocol and speed (JSON, as the pegen tables). Top level: table, protocol (i2c, uart, spi, onewire), title, source (the documents), source_note (the parts of the source that hold for every rule, and what the table chose itself), pins or frame (pins and format), rules.

Every rule has id, code (the witness's report code), kind, src (clause, table or figure of the source) and text. The kinds:

Kind Fields Meaning
min, max ns, from, to, when, after, unless the interval from the latest from event before a to event to that event is at least (at most) ns; when a state that must hold at the to event; the from event must be later than the latest of each after event; an unless event between the two voids the interval
order events, forbid an event of events while the state forbid holds
count event, max, reset more than max events since the latest reset event
framing, stop, start_glitch, noise, parity bits (stop) UART frame checks at the sample points of the frame model in the table's source_note

Events and states of each protocol:

Protocol Events States
I2C SCL_RISE, SCL_FALL, SDA_LOW (SDA changes while SCL is low), SDA_HIGH (while SCL is high), START, SR, STOP busy (between START and STOP), in_byte (1 to 8 clock pulses of the current byte done)
SPI CS_FALL, CS_RISE, SCK_EDGE, LATCH, SHIFT, DATA (MOSI or MISO changes while SS is low) frame, latch_phase (the last SCK edge latched), sck_active (SCK not at its CPOL level), partial (SCK edges of the transfer not a multiple of 16)
1-Wire FALL, RISE (every edge of DQ), PD_FALL, PD_RISE (the presence pulse: the first low that starts less than presence_window_ns after a reset pulse's rise), SLOT_FALL (every other fall: the master starts a time slot or a reset pulse), RST_RISE (the rise of a master low longer than slot_low_max_ns: a reset pulse), SLOT_RISE (the rise of any other master low) none

Changes in the same instant are ordered: an SDA change with an SCL rise is in the LOW before it (setup 0), with an SCL fall in the LOW after it; a SS fall comes first and a SS rise last; a data change comes before a latching edge and after a shifting edge; LATCH or SHIFT comes before the SCK_EDGE of the same edge; a 1-Wire FALL or RISE comes before its kind, so that after: RST_RISE leaves out the rise of a reset pulse itself.

Example (spec/i2c_sm.json):

{"id": "tHIGH", "code": 2, "kind": "min", "ns": 4000, "from": "SCL_RISE", "to": "SCL_FALL",
 "when": "busy", "unless": ["START", "SR"], "src": "Table 11, tHIGH",
 "text": "HIGH period of the SCL clock; the HIGH in which a START or repeated START is made is tHD;STA instead"}

onewire_std 9 Maxim DS18B20 data sheet, AC Electrical Characteristics and 1-Wire signaling (Initialization Procedure: Reset and Presence Pulses; Read/Write Time Slots), with Maxim AN126 for the master's standard-speed timing (editions not recorded, as in onewire.md): tRSTL at least 480 us, tPDHIGH 15 to 60 us, tPDLOW 60 to 240 us (two rules each), tRSTH at least 480 us (a slot started before the reset and presence sequence has ended), tSLOT at least 60 us, tLOW1/tLOWR at least 1 us, tREC at least 1 us

Values the source does not give are marked in the table as the table's own choice: the quarter-bit stop allowance of the UART tables (as in docs/witness.md), and for SPI, which has no standards-body specification (docs/spi.md), the SCK limit of 100 kHz and a setup of 1/4 SCK period. 100 kHz is the speed at which the generated SPI witness can judge every rule (see the witness section).

1-Wire has one open-drain line, and the line does not show who pulls it low. onewire_std therefore reads the roles from the order and the length of the lows, with two values in its frame, both the table's own choice and written in its source_note: the first low that starts less than 480 us (presence_window_ns, the master's receive time tRSTH) after the rise of a reset pulse is the presence pulse, and a master low longer than 120 us (slot_low_max_ns, the longest low of a time slot: tLOW0 and tSLOT at most 120 us) is a reset pulse, so a low of 120 to 480 us is a reset pulse that is too short. A time slot is measured from its fall to the master's next fall, the only marks on the line; that is also the table's choice. Left out because the line alone cannot show them: tLOW0 at least 60 us and tLOW1 at most 15 us (a write slot looks like a read slot in which a device holds the line low for a 0), tSLOT at most 120 us (the bus may idle between slots) and the master's sample point (tRDV).

test/test_i2c.py now reads its SPEC dictionary (Table 11 minimums and tVD;DAT, used by its bus monitor and by test_tmp102, test_i2c_sniff) from the three I2C tables instead of writing the numbers out; the values are the same.

The generator

tools/specgen.py (CLI, table loader, the two Python backends) and tools/specgen_fw.py (the witness backend):

python3 tools/specgen.py info spec/i2c_sm.json     # rules, what the witness checks, handling ticks
python3 tools/specgen.py all                       # every table -> firmware/gen, test/judges, tools/judges
python3 tools/specgen.py check                     # the committed files equal a fresh generation

The three backends share only the table:

  • cocotb judge. Turns the recorded changes into one ordered event stream (the extractor of the protocol) and emits one block of code per rule, with the number, the clause and the condition written out (if d < 4700: ...). UART: the frame model of the table, sampled in the middle of each bit from the start edge.
  • sigrok judge. A script that runs sigrok-cli with the protocol's decoder (i2c, uart, spi, onewire_link of libsigrokdecode 0.5.3, docs/sigrok.md says how to unpack it) on the VCD and takes the structure from it where the decoder has it: the START and STOP conditions, the UART frames and sample points, parity and framing errors, the SPI sampling edges and the decoded words, the 1-Wire reset and presence pulses. The rest (SCL falls, SDA changes in a LOW, edges between sample points, SS edges, the 1-Wire pulses that the decoder's own fixed windows leave out, counted as added) comes from the VCD. In the 1-Wire run below the decoder gave 72 of the 78 reset pulses and 59 of the 71 presence pulses. The rules are checked rule by rule with bisection over sorted event keys, not in a stream.
  • Witness. One template per protocol, written from the hand-written witnesses (same poll loop, same 24-tick tSU;DAT window, same log, counters and FIFO reports, same data memory layout, so test/test_witness.py runs on a generated file). A rule is recognised by its shape (kind, events, states), not by its name, and a rule of an unknown shape is refused rather than approximated. Thresholds come from the table in ticks of 40 ns (a minimum rounded up, a maximum down) and are written into the program; the first three I2C thresholds stay host-settable at 0x18 to 0x1A as in the hand-written witness. The 1-Wire template has no hand-written model: it catches every edge of DQ with waitp.frz, which stops the 16-bit timer in the tick the edge is seen, reads it whole, and then times the next interval in a short window (waitp.to.frz, 33 ticks for onewire_std: the 1 us thresholds of tLOW1 and tREC plus 8); when the window ends it restarts the timer for the long phase, loaded with 65,535 less the ticks since the edge, and handles the edge while the interval runs. A reading is therefore the whole interval to about a tick, and sums over several intervals (tSLOT, tRSTH) lose nothing. Thresholds are 16-bit immediates; a logged value is shifted down to fit a byte (units of 1 to 64 ticks, from the threshold, in the generated file's header and units of its .json).

Handling time. A polling witness measures an interval to its sampling only if the next change comes after it has finished with the last one. specgen_fw.handling() assembles the generated program and walks every path from each event handler back to the poll loop (one tick per instruction, a waitp.to its window, a report call the longest path of the report routine), with 0, 1 or 2 reports on the way. From that, the generator leaves out a rule whose violations it could not measure at the table's speed (the threshold less 6 ticks is shorter than the handling of the rule's from event), and the waveform generator spaces the edges of the waveforms meant for the witness. specgen.py info:

For 1-Wire the shortest thresholds are tLOW1 and tREC, 1 us = 25 ticks: their violations, less the 6-tick guard, all fall in the short window (from 6 ticks); the next shortest, tPDHIGH at least 15 us = 375 ticks from the reset pulse's rise, is four times the 93 ticks the witness needs after a rise (135 when the rise itself reports a short reset pulse), so the witness checks all nine rules at the table's speed. What it cannot time is two short intervals in a row (the second edge is handled with the first) and an interval between the end of the window and the handling time (it is read late, and the next one short): legal traffic has neither at AN126's values (lows of 6 us, recoveries of 10 us and more).

So the witness judges all of Standard-mode, but for Fast-mode and Fast-mode Plus only the rules whose violations it can still see: Fm and Fm+ traffic at the table's own speed is judged by the cocotb and the sigrok judges, and the witness is tried on Fm and Fm+ waveforms slowed to its handling times. This is the limit expected before the work for the short Fm+ rules at a 40 ns tick, now counted from the program instead of estimated.

Waveforms

tools/specwave.py builds seeded random waveforms from a table: I2C transfers (one or two, with repeated STARTs), UART frames (2 to 5, with ±2 % baud error on half of them), SPI transfers (1 to 3, 8 or 16 bits). One in four is built clean; the others have one or two rules of the table placed on purpose (planted). Every interval is drawn on one side of its table value, never closer to it than a guard: 30 ns for the exact judges, and for waveforms meant for the witness more than its reading error (I2C 8 ticks, 10 for tLOW, tSU;STA and tSU;STO and 12 for fSCL, which add up two or more timer segments; SPI 10 ticks; UART 12 ticks from every sample point and from the end of the stop window; see the witness errors in Results). After a UART noise event the line stays still for 36 ticks in waveforms meant for the witness (F8). Waveforms meant for the witness also keep each edge at least the witness's handling time after the one before (with the reports that edge makes), except an SDA change followed by the SCL rise inside the 24-tick window. The seed is recorded with every waveform (SPEC_SEED, default 20261112).

1-Wire waveforms (onewire_std): a reset pulse and its presence pulse (none in about one reset of seven), then 3 to 8 time slots (10 for the exact judges) of four kinds, write 0 (low 60 to 120 us), write 1 and read 1 (a low of 1 to 15 us), read 0 (a device holds the line low 15 to 45 us), and in three waveforms of ten a second reset with 1 to 3 more slots; the line is driven open drain. The guards are 30 ns for the exact judges and, for the witness, 8 ticks, 10 for tRSTH and tSLOT (sums of intervals) and around the two boundaries of the frame (120 us, 480 us). With the witness, every interval is either a planted short one (tLOW1 or tREC, 7 to 17 ticks, inside the short window, never two in a row) or at least the handling time of the edge before it (gaps, or gaps_short after a short interval, with the reports of those edges, from the generated program); onewire_wave refuses anything else and draws the waveform again. A waveform plants at most one of the minimum and the maximum of one time.

Cross-check

test/test_spec.py (cocotb, RTL) generates the witness and the cocotb judge from each table at run time, loads the witness on hart 1, and drives the waveforms onto the protocol pins from outside, as another device would (I2C open drain). It records every pin change and judges the record with the cocotb judge. For the waveforms meant for the witness the witness is restarted before each one; afterwards its counters must equal the cocotb judge's count per code, and its log (the last 8 reports) the judge's last violations, moment by moment (reports of one moment in any order), values within 6 ticks, or for tSU;DAT the witness's "too fast to time" reading of 11 or "same sample" reading of 0 against an exact setup of at most (reading + 4) ticks. The test fails on any difference. Every record is written to spec_<table>.jsonl.

tools/speccheck.py records <run dirs> writes each record as a VCD, runs the sigrok judge on it and compares: the cocotb and the sigrok judge must give the same violations in the same order, the same code and rule, times and values within 10 ns (UART 20 ns: the cocotb judge places a sample point at a fraction of a bit, sigrok's decoder at a whole sample).

1-Wire: the witness logs a time shifted down to a unit of 1 to 64 ticks (units of the generated .json, written into each record as wunits), so its log value is compared with the judge's value within 6 ticks plus one unit. speccheck.py records also prints, for 1-Wire, how many reset and presence pulses the sigrok judge took from the decoder and how many it added from the VCD (decoder_resets, added_resets, decoder_presence, added_presence, decoder_only), so that whoever runs it sees how much of the structure the third-party decoder gave.

cd test; T=$PWD; R=$T/sim_build/spec_i2c; mkdir -p $R; cd $R
PYTHONPATH=$T make -B -f $T/Makefile PWD=$T SIM_BUILD=$R/build COCOTB_TEST_MODULES=test_spec COCOTB_TEST_FILTER=test_spec_i2c
SIGROK_ROOT=<unpacked packages> python3 ../../../tools/speccheck.py records .   # docs/sigrok.md
python3 tools/speccheck.py offline i2c_sm --n 20     # cocotb against sigrok without a simulation

For 1-Wire the same with COCOTB_TEST_FILTER=test_spec_onewire (run folder spec_onewire), then speccheck.py records on that folder and speccheck.py offline onewire_std.

Disagreements found and fixed

In the order they were found. "Offline" is speccheck.py offline (cocotb against sigrok on generated waveforms, no simulation), before the first chip run; "chip" is test_spec on the RTL.

F1 offline i2c_sm seed 1 wave 10 (then 5 of 32 Sm, 12 of 32 Fm, 10 of 32 Fm+ waveforms) cocotb: byte_break (a STOP after 4 clock pulses of the address byte); sigrok: nothing sigrok judge libsigrokdecode 0.5.3 i2c/pd.py, decode(): in states FIND ADDRESS and FIND ACK it waits only for an SCL rise ({0: 'r'}), so a START or STOP inside the address byte or the acknowledge clock is never reported (the decoder printed the START at sample 329, nothing for the STOP at 6103). Limits

  • Expressiveness. A rule is an interval between two named events with conditions, an event forbidden in a state, or a count; the UART rules are fixed frame checks. There is no general temporal logic, and the events and states are fixed per protocol in the generator. A new rule of a known kind costs only a table line for the cocotb and sigrok judges, but the witness takes it only if its template knows the shape.
  • The witness's precision and speed. It samples every 4 ticks and reads times up to about 5 ticks short (Results). It judges every Standard-mode rule, but only tSU;DAT, byte_break and sda_twice of Fast-mode and Fast-mode Plus, and it is tried only on waveforms spaced to its handling times, which for Fm and Fm+ are slower than the tables allow. Fm and Fm+ traffic at full speed is judged by the cocotb and sigrok judges only.
  • The UART witness reports before it resynchronises (F8). After noise it spends 30 ticks reporting before it watches the line again, so an edge within that time is not taken as the next start edge, as the table's receiver would. The waveforms for the witness keep the line still there; a line that is not still can give a different frame from the next edge on.
  • Guards. Values closer to a table value than the guard (30 ns for the exact judges, 8 to 12 ticks for the witness) are not in the waveforms, so the judges are not compared there. The control with a changed table shows what happens on the line: a tSU;STO of exactly 5,000 ns against a limit of 5,000 ns was a violation for the witness only.
  • The sigrok judge takes from the decoders what they report correctly and the rest from the VCD: for I2C the START/STOP conditions the decoder does not look for (F1), for UART a fresh decode where the table's receiver starts over (F2), for SPI partial words by counting edges against the decoder's words. Its independence from the cocotb judge is therefore partial: two implementations of the same table over the same VCD, one of them leaning on a third-party decoder.
  • Sources. The SPI rules come from one controller's block description (S12SPIV4), since SPI has no standard; the SCK limit of 100 kHz and the 1/4-period setup, and the UART quarter-bit stop allowance, are the tables' own choices. The I2C tables use only UM10204 Rev. 7.0 Table 11 and 3.1.3 to 3.1.5 (no clock stretching rule, no 10-bit or Hs-mode rules, tf and tr not judged). The 1-Wire table takes its numbers from the DS18B20 data sheet's AC table and Maxim AN126, whose editions are not recorded in the repository (as for test_onewire), and was written without access to them on that day: the values are those docs/onewire.md, docs/ds18b20.md and test/test_onewire.py already use; one device's data sheet stands for the bus, and overdrive speed is not covered.
  • Hand-written files. firmware/witness_uart.pe keeps the longer stop window found in F5, and docs/witness.md still cites UM10204 Table 10 (the Rev. 6.0 number); neither was changed here.
  • 1-Wire: the decoder gives most, not all, of the structure. The sigrok judge takes 72 of the 78 reset pulses and 59 of the 71 presence pulses from onewire_link; the rest, all in waveforms with a planted tRSTL or tPDHIGH_max violation, come from the table's classification of the VCD, as do every time and every slot (the decoder's 428 "Bit" annotations are counted, not used). Where the decoder starts its annotations was first assumed and then corrected from its source after the first run (Results); the judge was not run against a decoder of another version.
  • 1-Wire: one line, roles inferred. The table reads who pulls the line low from the order and the length of the lows (a presence pulse is the first low within 480 us of a reset pulse's rise; a master low longer than 120 us is a reset pulse). A device that pulls the line low at another time, or a master that idles low, is classified by those rules, not by who did it. tLOW0 at least 60 us, tLOW1 at most 15 us, tSLOT at most 120 us and tRDV are not judged at all; a write 0 held 120 to 480 us is reported as a reset pulse that is too short.
  • 1-Wire witness. Two intervals in a row shorter than its short window (33 ticks) are not both timed, and an interval between the end of the window and the handling of the edge (up to 106 ticks after a fall, 93 after a rise, more with reports) is read late and the next one short; the waveforms keep out of both. Logged values are shifted down to fit a byte (units up to 64 ticks, 2.56 us); the thresholds are compared on the whole 16-bit reading.
  • Simulation only. The generated witnesses ran on the RTL in Icarus Verilog, not on the FPGA boards and not at gate level. On this tree test_spec is one of the 122 modules and 670 tests of the last full regression in verification.md section 1, 3 of 3 passing; test_spec_onewire was added after that regression and is not in it.
