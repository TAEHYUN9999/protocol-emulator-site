What stands on the other side of the bus when a firmware is judged, module by module. The count of protocols says how wide the firmware is; this page says how deep each check goes. It was written after an outside review asked for it: the firmware and the models that judge it have one author (an AI assistant, ai-use.md), so a shared misreading of a specification is the main risk, and a number of passing tests does not answer it.

Grades

Grade The chip's pins are judged by
A a real device, or third-party IP or a third-party decoder: sigrok's protocol decoders on the same waveform (sigrok.md), unmodified Xilinx AXI IIC and AXI Quad SPI in co-simulation and on the board (verification.md)
B a model or oracle the firmware's author did not write: third-party code used as the reference, a standard-library routine, or a judge whose decoding core is third-party
C the author's own model, with the pins compared against numbers of a cited specification or data sheet (timing limits, check values, published vectors)
D the author's own model only: expectations from the firmware's constants or from the same reading of the protocol

"Best" is the strongest evidence for any one test of the module, "bulk" what judges most of its tests. Where two grades were possible the lower one was taken.

These grades are stricter than the words of verification.md and faq.md, which call a model "independent" when it was written from the text of a specification rather than from the firmware (103 of 116 modules). Such a model is grade C here when the test compares cited numbers and grade D when it does not: by these definitions no module has a third-party or independently written judge for the bulk of its tests.

Method and limits

  • Two review sessions read the test modules (52 and 64 of them; the 116 modules that existed before this page), the protocol pages and the control scripts, read-only, without running a simulation. Long files were read in part (model classes, the assertions of every test, the header). The author's session then checked five of the reported gaps against the code; all five held. The other rows were not checked a second time.
  • The reviewers are AI sessions of the same kind as the author, with no part in writing the tests. That is a second reading, not an independent audit.
  • Six modules were added after the reading and after the regression of 0ba7952 (2026-11-12), whose 116 modules the first reading counted: test_clock_duty, test_reset_release, test_host_bringup, test_host_partial, test_petime and test_thirdparty. They are in the table since 2026-11-13, graded by the author's session from their headers and assertions (test_clock_duty and test_thirdparty with the assertions read, the other four from the header and the page), not by the review sessions.
  • Seven more modules came with the branches after mem512 and are neither in the table nor graded: test_rmii and test_spw (int4; line units against models the author wrote from the RMII and SpaceWire specifications), test_shm, test_core1 and test_iss_chip (int5; design checks of the shared SRAM and the second core, and the chip in lockstep with the reference model), test_reset_sync and test_status_ext (int5b; design checks). test_dly (int5 part D1; design checks of the delay lines against the liberty's tap delay) is an eighth and test_dly_train (int5 part D3; the training firmware of the delay lines) a ninth. No reviewer has read them; the 116 rows of the first reading still describe the modules on the RTL of 422ec10.
  • The 116 rows were not read again after the checks of 2026-11-13 were added. The grade of a row was changed only if the added checks changed what judges the pins by the definitions above, and none did; the Controls column names the planted bugs that came with them.
  • The gaps found on the way (tests without a control that can be rerun, models that mirror the firmware, assertions that cannot fail) are listed in test_gaps.md.
