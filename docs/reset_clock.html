Clock duty and reset release

Two assumptions about the clock and the reset pin that the RTL makes and the other pages did not state. An outside review of the design raised both. This page gives the facts read from src/, what was measured in RTL simulation and read from the static timing reports of the hardened layout, and what should change in a later RTL revision. Nothing in src/ was changed for this page. It was written for the RTL of 422ec10 (branches int3 and mem512, the hardened design); branch int5b has since added the reset synchronizer that section 2 recommends, and each part says which RTL its numbers are from.

Summary:

  • Clock duty. Four always blocks use the falling clock edge. At 40 to 60 % high time every simulated function still works: the 10BASE-T and USB frames carry the right bytes and both receivers pass their tests. The pin edges launched at the falling edge move by the high time minus 10 ns, so the transmitters' timing is no longer on the design's grid; the four transmitting modules fail at every duty other than 50 % for that reason (16 of 35 module runs), as the table below shows. The clock should be within 45 to 55 % for the Ethernet and USB units; the rest of the chip does not use the falling edge.
  • Reset, RTL up to mem512. There is no reset synchronizer: all 3,195 flip-flops take the rst_n pin as an asynchronous reset. With every flip-flop released in the same clock (any release time), the chip starts in the same state but for one free-running phase counter. If some flip-flops are released a clock later than others, the hart slot and four free-running counters can be left one clock apart; a soft reset from the host, which every documented start sequence sends, clears the slot. A two-flip-flop reset synchronizer is recommended for the next hardened revision.
  • Reset, branch int5b. The synchronizer is there (rst_m, rst_s in src/project.v). Of the 2,883 flip-flops of the RTL with the delay lines (44 taps) only rst_m can see a marginal release (2,717 on int5b; the delay lines of int5 part D1 add 166, dly.md). The state after the release is the same for all 40 release times, with no exception; if rst_m rises a clock late, every register starts one clock later together, and none is left a clock apart from another. That RTL is not hardened, so recovery and removal of rst_s have not been through static timing.

1. Falling-edge logic

grep -n "negedge clk" src/*.v gives four blocks and no other (line numbers of branch int5b; the line units added on int4, RMII and SpaceWire, have none):

Where Register Use
src/pe_eth_tx.v:146 b (1 bit) 10BASE-T transmitter: the pins are a ^ b with a on the rising edge, so the mid-cell transition of every other half bit (a half bit is 2.5 clocks) leaves at a falling edge
src/pe_usb_fs.v:379 b (2 bits) USB full-speed transmitter, the same way: a bit is 25/3 half clocks
src/project.v:66 rxn_m, rxn_s second synchronizer of the Ethernet receive pin ui[7], so the receiver has a sample every 10 ns
src/project.v:79 usbn_m, usbn_s (2 bits) the same for D+ and D-

Everything else, the core, the host link, the launcher and the capture among it, is clocked by the rising edge only, so its behaviour in clocks does not depend on the duty.

What the duty changes at the pins

With a high time H, an edge launched at the falling edge leaves H after the rising edge instead of 10 ns, so it moves by H - 10 ns against the edges launched at the rising edge. test/test_clock_duty.py drives the clock from a coroutine with 40, 45, 50, 55 and 60 % high time and measures the pins (RTL simulation, no gate or wire delay; 11 of 11 tests pass):

High time 10BASE-T: zero crossings 8.0 and 8.5 bit times after a crossing, deviation USB FS: consecutive transitions, paired transitions USB FS: SE0 width, last transition to SE0
40 % (8 ns) 2.0 ns 8.667 ns, 8.667 ns 168.0 to 172.0 ns, -3.33 to +8.67 ns
45 % (9 ns) 1.0 ns 7.667 ns, 7.667 ns 169.0 to 171.0 ns, -3.33 to +7.67 ns
50 % (10 ns) 0 6.667 ns, 6.667 ns 170.0 ns, -3.33 to +6.67 ns
55 % (11 ns) 1.0 ns 7.667 ns, 7.667 ns 169.0 to 171.0 ns, -3.33 to +7.67 ns
60 % (12 ns) 2.0 ns 8.667 ns, 8.667 ns 168.0 to 172.0 ns, -3.33 to +8.67 ns
Limit +-11 ns with the twisted-pair model, +-20 ns without (UNH-IOL 10BASE-T Embedded MAU Test Suite v5.3, tests 14.1.10 and 14.1.11, which cite IEEE 802.3 14.3.1.2.3) source: TDJ1 +-3.5 ns, TDJ2 +-4 ns; receiver must accept TJR1 +-18.5 ns, TJR2 +-9 ns (USB 2.0 Table 7-9) TFEOPT 160 to 175 ns, TFDEOP -2 to +5 ns

On the RTL of branch int5b the module passes 11 of 11 as well (2026-11-13, run alone, log outside the repository); the sweep of whole modules below was not repeated there.

In every run the 64-byte Ethernet frame decodes to the bytes sent with a good FCS, the six USB packets decode with their CRC16, and every pin edge leaves either at a rising clock edge or exactly H after it. Reading the table:

  • 10BASE-T transmit. The duty adds |H - 10 ns| of deterministic jitter, 2 ns at 40/60. That is small against the limit quoted above; the layout's own delays are not in this number.
  • USB full-speed transmit. The design does not meet the source jitter limits at any duty (usb_fs.md, faq.md); the duty adds |H - 10 ns| to the 6.67 ns of the 10 ns grid. What matters is the receiver's limit for paired transitions, 9 ns: at 40/60 the chip's 8.667 ns leaves 0.33 ns, and beyond 38.3 or 61.7 % (|H - 10 ns| above 2.33 ns) the paired jitter would pass it, before any delay of the layout or the board is counted.
  • Controls in the tests: the phase check run with H = 10 ns at another duty fails, as does test_eth's check of the 50 ns grid; the hand-driven clock at 50 % gives the same 1,756 pin edges, to the picosecond, as cocotb's Clock, and at 40 % does not.

Static timing of the half-cycle paths

The signoff STA of the hardened layout (422ec10, clock 20 ns, the flow's default constraints) assumes 50 % duty. Its worst paths by launch and capture edge, from the 1,000 worst paths per group and corner (evidence/duty_reset_20261112/sta_half_cycle.txt):

falling-edge flip-flop to rising-edge flip-flop 3.305 ns negative above a high time of 13.3 ns (66.5 %)
rising-edge flip-flop or input to falling-edge flip-flop at least 4.130 ns (not among the 1,000 worst paths of the slow corner; 5.945 ns and 8.556 ns are the worst in the typical corner) negative below a high time of at most 5.87 ns (29 %)

So the layout's setup timing between the two edges holds for 40/60 to 55/45 with the default output delay and for 40/60 to 60/40 without it. This is arithmetic on the 50 % reports, not a new STA run with another duty, and it does not cover the minimum pulse width of the clock at the flip-flops.

Output ports of run H5

The flow checks every output against a default output delay of 4 ns. Nothing outside the chip asks for those 4 ns: the protocols on these pins are asynchronous to anything the far side clocks, so what matters at the pins is the duty of the ports launched on the falling edge and the skew between the two wires of a pair, not an absolute delay. Run H5 (the RTL with 46 taps and the fine USB edges, before the four int6 blocks) gives, from the output port paths among the worst setup paths of each corner (evidence/physical_h5_14cdf53/sta_output_ports.txt, data arrival in ns from the rising clock edge at 0; the four pins below are launched at the falling edge, 10 ns):

Corner uo_out[6] TD+ uo_out[7] TD- pair difference uio_out[0] D+ uio_out[1] D- pair difference least slack against the 4 ns default
slow 13.691 13.551 0.140 13.319 13.235 0.085 2.059 ns (uo_out[6])
typ 12.391 12.305 0.086 12.173 12.134 0.039 3.359 ns
fast 11.662 11.607 0.055 11.508 11.494 0.014 4.088 ns

TD+ and TD- start from one falling-edge flip-flop, D+ and D- from two; the differences above are of the latest arrivals only (one path per port and corner, rising and falling not told apart), not a full skew analysis. The other outputs that appear in those reports start at the rising edge and arrive by 4.9 ns (typ), with slacks above 10 ns. Not in these numbers: the Tiny Tapeout output multiplexer, the pads and the board, which add delay and skew of their own, and the layout of the RTL in src/.

What is not known

  • The duty of the clock that the Tiny Tapeout demonstration board and its multiplexer deliver to the design (TODO(author): ask the organisers).
  • Gate-level simulation with SDF delays at another duty was not run; the simulations here are RTL.
  • The requirement is therefore stated as an assumption: the Ethernet and USB full-speed units assume a clock high time of 45 to 55 %. The other functions have no such requirement at RTL.

2. Reset release

The first four parts describe the RTL up to mem512 (422ec10), which has no reset synchronizer; their numbers are from evidence/duty_reset_20261112/test_reset_release.log, made with the module as it was then (git show 31851bb:test/test_reset_release.py). The last part describes branch int5b, which has one; test/test_reset_release.py of this tree carries the expected values of that RTL.

Facts

  • Every always block of src/*.v of that RTL is @(posedge clk or negedge rst_n) or, for the four above, @(negedge clk or negedge rst_n): all 3,195 flip-flops (the count of sg13cmos5l_dfrbpq_1 cells in the hardened netlist, evidence/physical_422ec10/synthesis-stats.txt, and of register bits in the RTL) are reset asynchronously by the rst_n pin through a buffer tree. There is no reset synchronizer and no other reset source; the soft reset of the host link (CTRL bit 2) is an ordinary synchronous signal.
  • The signoff STA does check recovery and removal on rst_n (worst recovery slack 3.801 ns in the slow corner, against the falling-edge flip-flops; worst removal slack 3.597 ns; evidence/physical_422ec10/sta_checks_*.rpt), but it times the pin as a synchronous input with the flow's default 4 ns input delay. On the demonstration board rst_n comes from a button or a microcontroller pin and is asynchronous to the clock, so those checks say nothing about it.
  • What can go wrong on silicon: when rst_n rises close to a clock edge, some flip-flops leave reset before that edge and some after it (or go metastable for a moment). Only a flip-flop whose input differs from its reset value at that edge can show the difference.

Release time swept, all flip-flops together

test/test_reset_release.py, RTL simulation: rst_n is released 0 to 19.5 ns after a rising clock edge in 0.5 ns steps (40 release times).

  • At every release time the outputs stay 0 for the next clocks, a program started without a soft reset reads the power-on state of verification.md (flags, timer, CRC, FIFO order), and a pin program started the same way puts its eight P0 edges at the same clocks counted from the first rising edge after the release. Control: with rst_n held low the program check fails.
  • The whole state (186 registers, 3,195 bits) four clocks after the release is the same for the 21 release times up to the falling edge (+0 to +10 ns). For the 19 after it (+10.5 to +19.5 ns) one register differs: u_usb.ph, the USB unit's bit phase counter. It runs freely modulo 25 half clocks from reset and is moved by transitions of D+/D-; the first "transition" it sees is the input synchronizers leaving their reset value 0 for the idle level of the pins, and the falling-edge and the rising-edge synchronizer take that level in the other order when the falling edge comes first. A soft reset does not clear it. By reading src/pe_usb_fs.v, not by a test: the transmitter loads the phase when a packet starts, and the receiver realigns it on every transition of the line, so the phase left by the reset is replaced by the first SYNC.
  • Control: the state five clocks after the release differs from the state four clocks after it in six registers (the free-running counters and the hart slot), so the comparison can see one clock.

Some flip-flops released one clock late

An RTL simulation releases every flip-flop at once, so the hazard is modelled: after the first clock edge a chosen register is put back to its reset value, as if its reset had been released one clock later than the others.

  • Which registers can show it. With four settings of the inputs, 14 registers leave their reset value at the first clock edge after the release; no other register changes at that edge, so no other can be affected: the input synchronizers pins_m, gpi_m, rxn_m, usbn_m and the host link's cs_s, sck_s, mosi_s (they take the level of their pin); the hart slot u_core.slot (it toggles every clock); the fetched word u_core.ins; and five free-running counters, u_capture.div (the capture's tick divider), u_eth.tmr (the link pulse timer), u_pel.tsl and u_pel.rem (the launcher's time since the last edge and its reservation count) and u_usb.ph. The test fails if the RTL changes this list. Control: a register outside the list (u_core.u_hart0.pc) held late changes nothing.
  • What stays different. Each of the 14 alone, each of the 91 pairs and all 14 together were held one clock late (106 cases); the state 12 clocks later is the reference state in 28 of them and differs in 78. The synchronizers of the host link, gpi_m, rxn_m and u_core.ins leave nothing behind. A counter held late stays one count behind (u_capture.div, u_eth.tmr, u_pel.tsl, u_pel.rem); a late pins_m or usbn_m moves u_usb.ph as above; a late u_core.slot leaves the two harts on the other clocks.
  • What the host sees. With all 14 late, the program started without a soft reset still reads the power-on state, and the pin program started without a soft reset has every P0 edge one clock later than the reference: the hart slot is flipped. After a soft reset the slot is the reference's again (the pin program started with a soft reset first has its edges at the same clocks, shift 0), and four registers still differ: u_capture.div, u_pel.tsl, u_pel.rem, u_usb.ph.
  • Why those four are not expected to matter (by reading the RTL, not proved): each is re-anchored by the event it serves before it is used. div is cleared when a capture is armed, tsl restarts at every reference edge, rem is loaded when a launch is armed, ph is loaded at the start of a transmission and realigned by the line when receiving. They are counters whose phase against the outside world is arbitrary in any case, since the host is not synchronous to the chip clock.

Limits of the model: a multi-bit register is held late as a whole, not bit by bit (the bits of a counter released in different clocks are not covered); two clocks of difference and metastable values are not modelled; the functional checks after the release are the two small programs above, not the regression. The formal power-on proof (verification.md) starts from the reset state with every flip-flop released together.

Assessment and recommendation

  • The reviewer's statement is right: there is no reset synchronizer, and the documents did not say so.
  • With that RTL, a start sequence that sends a soft reset before running a hart (run_program of test/test.py and the FPGA self test script fpga/gen_script.py do: CTRL with bit 2, then CTRL with the run bits) removes the one difference found that changes pin timing, the hart slot. A host that runs a hart straight after power-on without a soft reset can find the harts on either clock phase after a marginal release.
  • Recommended for the next hardened RTL revision, not done on mem512 and done on branch int5b (next part): a two-flip-flop reset synchronizer (asynchronous assertion, synchronous release) in src/project.v feeding every block, with the falling-edge flip-flops released from a stage clocked so that their first edge is defined; then the recovery and removal checks of the STA apply to a signal that is really synchronous, and the power-on proof should be rerun. Until then: hold rst_n low for at least a few clocks, release it once, and send a soft reset before the first START.
  • Related, from the same review: the outputs uio_out, uio_oe and uo_out[7:6] are selected by configuration bits after the registers (src/project.v), so the configuration should be written before the pins are driven.

Branch int5b: with the reset synchronizer

Facts, read from src/project.v of this tree:

  • rst_n resets two flip-flops asynchronously, rst_m and rst_s. At every rising clock edge rst_m takes 1 and rst_s takes rst_m. Every other always block of src/project.v and every block below it is reset by rst_s, not by the pin. So rst_n low still resets everything at once, with or without a clock, and the blocks leave reset at the second rising edge after rst_n rises (architecture.md; test/test_reset_sync.py checks that time to the picosecond).
  • Of the release of rst_n only rst_m can show a marginal edge: it is the one flip-flop whose input (1) differs from its reset value at that edge. rst_s has 0 at its input then. The worst case is therefore rst_m rising at the second edge instead of the first (or going metastable, with a clock to settle before rst_s takes it; no failure rate was computed).
  • The RTL with the delay lines (44 taps) has 2,883 flip-flop bits in 422 registers, as test/test_reset_release.py reads them from the source and the hierarchy: the 2,717 bits in 413 registers of int5b and the 166 bits in 9 registers of the delay lines (src/pe_dly.v, branch int5 part D1). No kept netlist of this RTL exists to count cells in; on int5b Yosys 0.9 (read_verilog, proc, opt_clean, the hierarchy kept) gave the same 2,717 bits, all with an asynchronous reset, and the hierarchical synthesis of the delay line RTL has 166 flip-flop cells more than that of int5b. The data memory and the frame buffer are no longer flip-flops (architecture.md), and there is a second core.

test/test_reset_release.py on this RTL, 2026-11-13 (in a copy of the tree of eabbb5e, Icarus Verilog 11.0, cocotb 2.0.1; 4 of 4 pass; log outside the repository; the numbers below are those of the RTL with the delay lines, and where they differ from int5b the earlier number is given). Its expected values were changed from those of the parts above for three reasons, each read from the failing run first: the register finder took a variable of an always block in src/pe_shm.v for a flip-flop, the flip-flop count was that of the hardened int3 netlist, and the exposed set and the staggered result were those of an RTL without the synchronizer.

  • Release time swept. For the 40 release times the outputs stay 0, the program started without a soft reset reads the power-on state, and the pin program puts its eight P0 edges at the same clocks counted from the first rising edge after the release (1952 to 2094, the same clocks as on the RTL above). Control: with rst_n held low the program check fails.
  • State. The whole state (422 registers, 2,883 bits) four clocks after the release is the same for all 40 release times. The one exception of the RTL above, u_usb.ph, is gone: rst_s always rises at a rising clock edge, so the falling-edge synchronizers always come first. After a soft reset no register differs. Control: one clock later 16 registers differ (14 on int5b).
  • First edges. With the four settings of the inputs: nothing moves at a falling edge before rst_s; at the first rising edge after rst_n only rst_m changes, at the second only rst_s. After rst_s, 27 registers leave their reset value at their first edge: the 14 of the RTL above, the slot and the fetched word of the second core (u_core1.slot, u_core1.ins), the zero-fill counters of the two shared SRAMs (u_shm.ic, u_shm1.ic), the SpaceWire unit's disable counter (u_spw.rcnt) and, since the delay lines, their calibration counter and the flip-flop it toggles (u_dly.cal_cnt, u_dly.cal_src), since the quiet-line rule of the delay lines their input samples (u_dly.in_m), and since the MBIST collars' falling-edge pin stage (collar v2) its write flag in each of the five collars (u_mbc0.o_w to u_mbc4.o_w), at the first falling edge: element 0 is a write, while the BIST pins stay 0 because they are gated by o_act, which stays 0 until a self test runs. They are released together by rst_s, a flip-flop output that changes at a rising edge, a whole clock before the next rising edge and half a clock before the falling edge of rxn_m and usbn_m. The test fails if the RTL changes either list.
  • rst_m one clock late (put back to 0 after its first edge, the model of a marginal release). The state 13 clocks after the release is the reference's state after 12 clocks in all 422 registers: the chip as a whole starts one clock later. Compared at the same clock, 12 registers differ (the free-running counters, the slots, the zero-fill counters; 11 on int5b, the twelfth is u_dly.cal_cnt). The program started without a soft reset still reads the power-on state, and the pin program started without a soft reset has every P0 edge one clock later (1953 against 1952), since the host counts from the release and the harts from rst_s. After a soft reset the pin program's edges are at the reference's clocks (shift 0) and five registers still differ: the same four as above, for the reasons given there (u_capture.div, u_pel.tsl, u_pel.rem, u_usb.ph), and u_dly.cal_cnt, the counter that starts a calibration measurement every 64 clocks; it runs freely from reset, so its measurements come one clock later, which no pin shows. Control: u_core.u_hart0.pc held late changes no register.
  • Controls on the RTL (test/controls.sh): with rst_n handed to the blocks directly (reset_no_sync_state) the state test fails on u_usb.ph, as on the RTL above; with rst_s set at the first edge (reset_one_stage_exposure) the first-edges test fails, a register moving at a falling edge before the second rising edge.

What this does not show: a zero-delay RTL simulation cannot show a recovery or removal violation or a metastable value. Whether rst_s and its buffer tree meet a clock (half a clock for the falling-edge flip-flops) is for the static timing of a hardened layout: the first hardening run of int5b (H1, without the delay lines, results outside the repository) reports no timing violation, and its recovery and removal slacks and an SDF run of this module on its netlist are in verification.md, section 6; the module was not run at gate level with the delay lines. The start sequence above still holds: hold rst_n low for a few clocks, release it once, and send a soft reset before the first START, because a late rst_m moves the harts by one clock against a host that counts from the release.

Reproduce

cd test
make -B COCOTB_TEST_MODULES=test_clock_duty       # 11 tests, about 20 s
make -B COCOTB_TEST_MODULES=test_reset_release    # 4 tests, about 9 minutes (about 10 on `int5b`)
./run_duty.sh $TMPDIR/duty 3                         # 7 modules x 5 duties, about 25 minutes
CLK_DUTY=45 CLK_HAND=1 make -B COCOTB_TEST_MODULES=test_eth_rx   # any module at another duty
