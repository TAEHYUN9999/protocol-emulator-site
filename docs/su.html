src/pe_su.v is a general shift unit: the firmware describes how a bit looks on the pins (clock phase, lanes, bit order, a waveform table, a stuffing rule, a sample point) and hands it words; the unit moves the bits at clock resolution (20 ns) instead of one bit per instruction (40 ns tick, three to seven instructions per bit without it). It is not a protocol block: nothing in it knows SPI, CAN or WS2812. Since branch int7 the chip has one, and the submitted chip (int8_su, the shift unit on c74b3267, since 2026-11-20) has it too, in core 0 (u_su in src/project.v), used by the hart that the host names with SU_OWN; In the chip describes the integration and its verification, the other sections the unit itself. The unit in the chip is the smaller variant of Area and timing: two entries per FIFO and sample delays of 0 to 3 units.

Why: 113 of the 123 golden firmware files of 2026-11-14 (129 of the 152 now) use shout/shin, one bit per instruction, and that is what stops SPI master at 3.125 MHz (spi.md), the quad SPI device at 1.25 MHz (w25q_quad.md), I3C at 500 kHz and the integer-tick rates of I2S, S/PDIF and XY2-100 (RTL_NEXT.md 1.1, kept outside the repository).

Numbers below are labelled [measured] (a run of the tests or tools named next to them) or [calculated] (arithmetic on measured or specified values).

Interface

Hart side

One register port, used by one hart (the owner, chosen by the host):

Port Width Meaning
wr 1 write register sel with wdata (already qualified by the owner's execute slot)
rd 1 read register sel (rdata, combinational); a read of RXD pops
sel 5 register number, table below
wdata 8
rdata 8
stall 1 this access cannot complete now and has no effect: a push into a full TX FIFO, a pop from an empty RX FIFO, a SYNC read while work is left
clear 1 soft reset: every register to its reset value, FIFOs empty, pins released

stall has the same contract as the host FIFO instructions txb/rxb: the blocking form repeats the access in the next tick, the .nb form gives up with Z = 1.

Pin side

Port Width Meaning
pins_in 8 P0 to P7 after the two flip-flop synchronizer (pins_s in project.v) and one more rising-edge flip-flop (su_pin_s, retiming, 2026-11-26): data lanes in. Pin to the unit's decision: 2 synchronizer clocks + 1 retiming clock = 3 clocks. Turn-on boundary: a pin change is counted when it reaches the unit, 3 clocks later, at or after the clock E that turns the unit on, so changes from about E - 3 count (E - 2 before the retiming; sim 40_BOARD_EVIDENCE_20261119/g5s_sim_smoke_20261127, 535cab7e against d03e8749, post-hoc judge, posthoc_note.txt)
pins_pre 8 P0 to P7 after the first synchronizer flip-flop (pins_m) and the retiming flip-flop (su_pin_m): the clock pin and the select pin take their second stage inside the SU, after the pin mux, so they have the timing of pins_in with the 8-way mux in front of a flip-flop
pin_out, pin_oe 8 + 8 flip-flops; the SU's own drive of P0 to P7

The SU uses up to four data lanes out (from txp), up to four in (from rxp), a clock pin (out in the master modes, in for the device mode) and a select pin (in, device mode). It never changes a pin's output and enable bits at one edge where the state in between would be low: the same rule as the core's pad registers (dual_role.md), built into its own output flip-flops (release to high moves the output first, high to release the enable first).

Half-clock units

(Moved from the draft drafts/pblock_g4.md of branch docs_g4, 8094c007, with the names of this document.) A unit is N + k/M clocks (DIVI, DIVK, DIVM, DIVH). With CLKC bit 1 (hm) the same numbers count half clocks: N + k/M half clocks, so a boundary can fall on a falling clock edge. DIVI 0 counts as 256 and DIVI 1 as 2. A one-segment bit (W01/W11 = 0) needs a unit of at least 4 half clocks (DIVI 0 or at least 4).

  • Timer (pe_su.v): hrem is the number of half clocks from this clock's rising edge to the next boundary; a unit ends one clock before its boundary (hrem 2: the next rising edge; 3: the falling edge after it, h_fall). The comparisons are kept in flip-flops (hlo, hodd), so the pin path has no comparator.
  • Pins: the unit's output for a pin is pin_out ^ pin_x. pin_out changes only at rising edges, pin_x only at falling edges. When a boundary is on a falling edge, the rising edge keeps the pins and records in xpend (a rising-edge flip-flop) the pins that change; the falling edge flips them (pin_x <= pin_x ^ xpend, exported as pin_flip). Only one flip-flop of a pin changes at an edge, so the pin has no glitch. Enables change only at rising edges.
  • Examples: 10BASE-T's half bit is 2.5 clocks = 5 half clocks (hm, DIVI 5), so the middle of each cell is on a falling edge; USB full speed's bit is 25/3 half clocks (hm, DIVI 8, k 1, M 3).
  • Measured on the chip (test_su_chip_half_clock_pads, int9_line 6bbb1ea): with hm and DIVI 5 the clock pin P5 changes every 5 half clocks (2.5 clocks), every other change half a clock off the rising edges (24 of 50); without hm every 10 half clocks, all on rising edges; no pad rule violation and no zero-width pulse.

Settings during a frame (since 2026-11-24, checked by test_su_pinchk): a DIVI write registered at rising edge t applies to the units that start at t + 1 clock or later; a unit that starts earlier keeps the old length. The one-segment choice of W01/W11 = 0 is made when a bit starts, with the DIVI and CTRL in force for its first unit, and held for that bit (W01/W11 themselves are written between frames only). In test_su_pinchk the write was moved over every phase of the unit grid (full units of 3 clocks, half-clock units of 5 half clocks); of the rules 'from t + n half clocks' only n = 2 fits all frames (n = 1 fails three, n = 3 nine).

Each TX FIFO entry (two, 15 bits) keeps the kind, CTRL.lanes, LEN.wlen and CTRL.lsb of the moment it was pushed (the data is stored in send order). A lane change, a turnaround from driving to receiving or a change of word length therefore lands exactly between two words, also in device mode where the other side's clock does not wait. The RX FIFO holds two bytes.

Instructions

The SU needs a blocking push and pop, so ld/st alone do not fit: they never stall, and every address of the load/store space is taken (data memory, frame buffer; SpaceWire already overlays frame bytes). Polling a status byte before each push costs three ticks per byte, which is the bottleneck the SU removes. Two instruction forms are enough, both in the opcode 0xF space that int6 leaves free with ins[4] = 1 (built in int7, src/pe_core.v; tools/peasm.py takes sel as a number, a .equ name or a register name of the table above, in any case):

Instruction Encoding Operation
sur[.nb] rd,sel 1111 0ddd 0nS1 cccc rd = SU register {S, cccc}; a read of RXD pops, of SYNC waits
suw[.nb] sel,rs 1111 0sss 1nS1 cccc SU register {S, cccc} = rs; TXD, TXO, RXW push
  • ins[7] = write, as stx and dlyw in int5; ins[6] (n) = .nb; ins[5] (S) is bit 4 of sel; ins[4] = 1 separates the SU from int5's ldx/stx (ins[4:3] = 00) and dlyr/dlyw (ins[4:3] = 01); ljmp/lcall use ins[11] = 1.
  • Flags: Z = 0 when the access completes (also the blocking form, like txb/rxb); the .nb form sets Z = 1 and does nothing when it would stall. C is kept. One tick when it does not stall.
  • Only the owner hart reaches the SU. For the other hart both forms do nothing: sur writes 0, the .nb form sets Z = 1, and neither stalls.
  • No existing word changes meaning: these words were no-ops in int6 (the 0xF allocation of tickgen.md), and test/check_asm_compat.py --rev 0346f7f assembles every firmware file to the same words [measured: 145 files, 32,857 words].

Operation

Units and the fraction

All master-mode timing counts units. A unit is N + k/M clocks: unit u lasts N + floor(u k / M) - floor((u - 1) k / M) clocks, u = 1, 2, ..., so every unit is N or N + 1 clocks, any w consecutive units contain floor(w k / M) or one more long units, and M units take exactly M N + k clocks (a Bresenham accumulator; the carry is a one-clock extension of the unit, so no adder follows the compare). k and M are 12 bits, enough for the audio clocks exactly:

Words

A TX entry is 1 to 8 bits on 1, 2 or 4 lanes. MSB first sends bit n-1 first and puts the higher bit of each group on the higher lane (quad SPI: IO3..IO0 = bits 7..4, then 3..0); LSB first sends bit 0 first, lane j = bit j of the group. The entry is converted to send order at the push, so the engine only shifts one way. A receive-only entry (RXW) clocks wdata + 1 words of wlen bits with the data lanes released: one push for a 256-byte read burst.

The RX side assembles every received sample of a word that receives (TXD, RXW, not TXO) into words of rxlen bits, right aligned, MSB or LSB first by CTRL.lsb. When the stream ends (master idle, device deselected) a partial word is pushed with the bits it has. A finished word reaches the RX FIFO three clocks after its last sample strobe (capture, assembly, FIFO write); a full FIFO drops it and sets over.

Clocked mode

A bit has two segments of one unit each. Segment 0 shows the data lanes (and moves the clock pin for CPHA 1), segment 1 moves the clock pin; the clock pin rests at CPOL. That is SPI mode 0 to 3 by cpol/cpha. SCK = 50 MHz / (2 (N + k/M)): 25 MHz at N = 1.

Samples are taken smp units after the start of segment 1, through a three-stage line that carries, with each strobe, the tags of the bit it belongs to (receive or not, lane count, recessive bit sent). A strobe delayed past its bit for round-trip compensation therefore still lands in the right word and lane count. The window that read MISO right against an SPI slave answering 4 ns after its edge, through the two-stage synchronizer [measured, test_spi_modes]: smp 1 to 2 at N = 1 (25 MHz), 0 to 1 at N = 2, 3, 5 and 2 + 1/3, in all four modes. In the chip the clock pin passes the pad register as well, one clock more, and the window moves by one clock [measured, test_su_chip_spi_master]: smp 2 to 3 at N = 1, 1 to 2 at N = 2.

External clock mode (device)

The clock pin is an input. CPHA 0 samples on the leading edge and shows the next bit on the trailing edge; CPHA 1 shows the next bit on the leading edge and samples on the trailing edge (the first bit is already out at select, so the first leading edge does not shift). With early the next bit is shown at the sample edge itself, which gives the master a whole period instead of half of one. With sel_en the select pin (active low) frames transfers: deselected, the lanes are released and the bit counters restart; at select the first bit of the first entry is shown at once. A word is loaded at a launch edge when the previous one has ended, with its own lane count: a quad SPI device puts its first data nibble out after the last dummy clock without any hart timing.

Delay from the master's edge to the data change: 48 to 58 ns in the standalone wrapper (two synchronizer clocks plus the output flip-flop) [measured, test_device_spi]. The first bit appears two to three clocks after the select edge in the same way, so the master must leave about three clocks (60 ns) plus its setup time between select and its first sampling edge (the bench left two half periods, 70 ns at 14.3 MHz).

Stuffing, NRZI, read-back

With STUF.tx and one lane, a stuff bit is inserted after n equal bits (rule 0, CAN: the stuff bit starts the next run), after n ones (rule 1, USB, HDLC) or after n zeros (rule 2), also at the end of the stream. STUF.rx removes them on receive with the same rule and sets serr for a wrong one; SMP.nrzi decodes NRZI first. Stuffing runs across entries and restarts with each frame. CLKC.rb compares each sent recessive (1) bit with the line at its strobe: a 0 sets lost, and with rbstop lane 0 stays recessive afterwards while the SU keeps clocking and receiving (CAN arbitration).

Flow control

TXD/TXO/RXW pushes stall while the TX FIFO is full, RXD pops while the RX FIFO is empty, SYNC while anything is left. In master mode the engine starts when an entry is there and stops at the end of a word when none is; with CTRL.frame that stop sets under (the firmware clears frame after its last push). Idle data lanes keep the direction of the last word: driven at didle after a driven word (and after reset), released after a receive-only word, so a device still driving after a read burst is not driven against. Changing CTRL lanes between pushes is the intended use; changing the mode, the stuffing or the divider while words are queued is not supported.

Firmware sketches

The chip test test/test_su_chip.py has the full programs (SPI master, Quad SPI read, WS2812, UART send and receive); the loop of the Quad SPI read:

    suw CTRL,r_en1        ; one lane
    suw TXO,r_6b          ; instruction
    suw TXO,r_a2 ; suw TXO,r_a1 ; suw TXO,r_a0
    suw TXO,r_ff          ; 8 dummy clocks
    suw CTRL,r_en4        ; four lanes for the next pushes
    suw RXW,r_nm1         ; n words, lanes released
loop:
    sur r1,RXD            ; stalls until a byte is in
    st  r1,[r2]
    addi r2,1
    djnz r3,loop
    sur r0,SYNC
    high CS

WS2812 frame: configure the table once, then ld r1,[r2]; addi r2,1; suw TXO,r1; djnz per byte (a byte is 9.6 us of line time, about 240 ticks, so the hart has time for other work).

What PIO does that the SU does not: any sequence per bit (a program can mix waits, jumps and pin moves), 32-bit words, DMA, eight engines. What the SU does differently: the timing of a bit is a property of the unit, not of a program, so it is checked once for every program (the formal properties below hold whatever the firmware does), and the firmware keeps the protocol decisions at its exact tick timing (twait, late flag) while the unit moves bits at clock resolution.

Formal (formal/run_formal_su.sh, formal/pe_su_props.sv)

Every input is free (hart port, pins, clear, reset after the first clock), except that the hart writes sel 0 to 23 only (pe_su_props.sv: if (wr) assume (sel < 5'd24); with HM_PROPS also 29 and 30), and rx_on = 1, fr_drop = 0, the symbol layer inputs = 0 (see Reach of the formal properties). Properties:

  1. The unit is on exactly when CTRL, rebuilt in the wrapper from the hart's writes, says en and mode != 3. No pin is driven after reset until then, no enable one clock after it is off, no output bit two clocks after.
  2. Pad rule: no pin changes its enable and output bits at one edge, except (0, 1) -> (1, 0).
  3. FIFOs: counts in range, no pop from empty, no push into full; one freely chosen entry of each FIFO is followed and leaves with its value after exactly the entries ahead of it (so no word is lost or reordered while the FIFO does not overflow); a finished RX word is either stored or recorded in over.
  4. A unit lasts exactly the length loaded at its start, which is N or N + 1 clocks (N when the fraction is off).
  5. (bounded) With rule 0 no n + 1 equal bits are shown, with rule 1 no n + 1 ones, under the assumptions that STUF is written only between frames, one lane, master modes.

Frame rules (sub-unit pe_frule, G10; standalone, not yet connected)

src/pe_frule.v judges received frames by settings only (PE_SU_ABSORB_PLAN 28.3): four rule lines that compare the bytes at a position with set values, and one lane rule that waits for a lane pattern held for n samples. It replaces the fixed checks of the old receivers (a destination of six bytes equal to the own address or all ones; dropping a frame whose first byte has one value; a line state held long enough) with one form that has no frame format in it. It sits in page 2 of the indirect window (XIDX[7:6] = 1) and is not yet wired into pe_su.

Ports: the common ones of 28.1 (clk, rst_n, clear, x_we, x_addr[5:0], x_wd, x_rd combinational) and rb_v, rb_d[7:0], rb_idx[10:0] (a received byte, its position), rf_start, rf_end (one clock each), ln_v, ln[1:0] (a lane sample) in; f_drop, f_hit[3:0], f_rearm, f_ln_ev out.

Why this layout (area): the compare bytes are a pool of eight slots that the four lines share through BASr, not six bytes per line. Per-line storage would need 4 x 6 x 16 = 384 flip-flops for values and masks; the pool needs 128, about 12,500 µm² less [estimate: 256 flip-flops at 49 µm², the dfrbpq_1 of the measurement below]. Eight slots hold one six-byte first-n-bytes filter and two one-byte rules, or four two-byte rules, and two lines may read the same slots at different positions (the same value at two places). The comparison is done once per slot (eight comparators of the byte at hand), and each line only picks the result of its slot (an 8:1 choice of two bits), instead of an 8:1 choice of a 16-bit value and mask per line. clear writes 0 only to the enables (len, LRUN): every rule is then off, as after reset, and the other bytes, which no rule reads while it is off, need no clear gate. Positions are 6 bits (the frame buffer stores 64 bytes); the 3-bit len field allows 7 (the plan asks 1 to 6, 7 costs nothing). The action bits sit at the same places in CFGr and LPAT ([6:4]).

Behaviour

  • Line r (on when len != 0) compares the bytes at positions pos .. pos + len - 1 with slots base .. base + len - 1 under the masks. It matches when all len bytes arrived and each equals its value, or, with ones, each has all its masked bits at 1 (the whole run, not a mix). It fires on a match, or with neg when it does not match, which includes a frame that ended before the line's last byte. A firing line with drop holds f_drop from the clock after its last byte (or in the rf_end clock when it fires only there) through the rf_end clock; with rearm it gives f_rearm in the rf_end clock; with report it sets f_hit[r] at rf_end. f_hit holds until the next rf_end (and clear).
  • In the chip (pe_su, emode 2) a rearm ends the frame one clock after f_rearm (a flip-flop, fr_rq): rf_end is the unit's frame end and the rules give f_rearm in their rf_end clock, so a same-clock use was a combinational loop (2050ed26 to 8101a224, found by the board build; tools/loop_check.py now checks the top for loops without hardening).
  • A first-n-bytes filter (own value or all ones, else drop and hunt for the next frame) is one line: len n, ones, neg, drop, rearm. Dropping frames that start with a value without a report is len 1, rearm (and drop, to keep the buffer).
  • Lane rule (on when LRUN != 0): an ln_v sample with (ln & mask) == (value & mask) counts, any other ln_v sample restarts the count; the n-th counting sample in a row is the event, once per run: report gives f_ln_ev and rearm gives f_rearm, both in that clock, drop raises f_drop from the next clock to the next rf_end or rf_start. A write of LPAT or LRUN restarts the count.
  • Frame edges: a byte in an rf_end clock belongs to the ending frame; rf_start in that clock starts the next frame empty; a byte in an rf_start clock alone is the new frame's. rf_start without a preceding rf_end (a frame given up) restarts the lines.
  • Settings are meant to be written between frames; a write during a frame takes effect at once.

For pe_su (connection, sub3): f_drop is meant to gate the buffer write of the byte in the same clock (it is a register except for the rf_end clock part); f_rearm and f_drop are valid in the rf_end clock, so the end decision can be taken there; f_hit is a register, valid from the clock after rf_end.

Limits

  • One unit for the chip, in core 0: harts 2 and 3 have none, and the two harts of core 0 share it through the host's choice of owner.
  • When a second Protocol Block is added in core 1 (user decision 2026-11-26), it is built with ADV = 0: the G4 functions only (no edge timing, no page 1 or 2: LEN bits 7 and 3 read 0 and their writes are ignored, sel 24 to 28 are always the immediate table and LCFG, STAT bit 0 and FLAG bits 5 and 6 read 0). The shared sub-modules (pulse, frame rules, symbol layer) serve the core 0 Protocol Block only. Two Protocol Blocks can still run two different protocols at once.
  • The pins toggle at up to 25 MHz (SCK at N = 1). Whether the Tiny Tapeout mux and pads pass that is not checked.
  • No clock recovery: the master modes do not resynchronise on the bus's edges, so a receiver of an asynchronous stream (CAN from other nodes, USB, UART at high rates) works only in read-back of its own frame or from a frame start that the hart catches (the UART receive of test_su_chip_uart: the hart waits for the start bit, then pushes one RXW).
  • Device mode is two to three clocks from the master's edge to the data change in the standalone wrapper, and 62 to 80 ns in the chip [measured, simulation of fpga/zcu104_demo/su_spi_slave]: with early a master gets the bit a whole period later, so 9 MHz works and 18 MHz (55.6 ns) does not; MDIO's 0 to 30 ns PHY output time is still not met.
  • Stuffing and destuffing work on one lane. Lengths that are not a multiple of the lane count send padding in the last group.
  • The table has two segments per bit value. Parallel WS2812 strings (one string per lane, common timing) need a three-segment form and are not supported.
  • CAN FD's fixed stuff bits in the CRC field and the stuff count are not supported; its CRC-17/21 needs a wider CRC than the hart's 16 bits (RTL_NEXT.md 2.9).
  • Configuration changes other than CTRL.lanes/LEN between pushes take effect at once, also on queued words. A change of SU_OWN hands the new owner whatever the unit holds, except the page: in the clock the owner changes, LEN bit 7 goes back to 0 (since G5, 2026-11-25), so the new owner's sel 24..28 reach page 0 until it sets the page itself.
  • Two FIFO entries: a firmware loop that cannot take a received byte every byte time of the line loses words on receive-only bursts (over); the four-instruction loop keeps up at four lanes and 12.5 MHz, a longer loop needs a slower SCK. Sample delays beyond 3 units are gone (the read slot of 1-Wire is sampled at 9 us).
  • Not run: the gate level and the delay lines in front of the unit's pins (enabled lines).
  • On the FPGA board as SPI master, mode 3, at 12.5 MHz against an STM32G474 (bit f6e7c3de, overview), also for 24 hours at SMP 1: MISO right in 1,589,690 of 1,589,702 transfers, the 12 wrong all within 190 s, each the G474's reply shifted by whole bits (1 to 8), as from a slave without NSS that counted extra SCK edges; in a 30-minute rerun an extra SCK edge between transfers, on the line the chip releases, shifted one transfer by one bit on a slave without NSS and was ignored by a slave with NSS (analysis); that this was the cause of the 24-hour errors is not confirmed; MOSI right in 1,508,596 of the 1,508,599 lines the G474 printed, with no lines recorded for 4,410 s (evidence). The window of right sample delays moves with SCK: SMP 0 and 1 at 6.25 MHz, 1 and 2 at 12.5 MHz, only 3 at 25 MHz, which leaves 25 MHz no margin. With the chip's CS on the G474's hardware NSS (2026-11-21): MISO right 20 of 20 with CS low, ff 10 of 10 with CS held high, and the G474 took no byte without CS (evidence). As SPI device, mode 3 with early, against the G474 as master (2026-11-22, bit 0eeefc8d): at 9 MHz both directions right in 260 of 260 transfers with the G474's pins at medium speed (MOSI 59 and MISO 57 of 60 at very high speed); at 18 MHz and without early MISO one bit late as in simulation, and MOSI wrong too, which a model of crosstalk from MISO onto SCK reproduces (evidence). Table mode, stuffing and the other protocols of this page ran in simulation only.

Synchronizer pins_in = pins_s, pins_pre = pins_m of project.v

Pulse sub-module (src/pe_pulse.v, G8)

A periodic pulse between frames and a minimum gap after a frame (PE_SU_ABSORB_PLAN 28.2). The module is standalone here; pe_su connects it through the page 2 window (XIDX[7:6] = 0, XIDX[5:0] = x_addr, 28.1). Every number is a register; all are 0 after reset and after clear (soft reset), and 0 means off. No time value is built into the RTL.

Changing the period (director decision 2026-11-25, test_pe_pulse.test_shorter_period_order): the timer counts from reset, clear or a selected restart whether or not a period is set, and a pulse is due when the count reaches P - 1. Firmware that changes P writes CTL with RS_WR = 1 first (that write itself does not restart: it uses the old CTL) and then PER: each PER write restarts the count, so the first pulse comes exactly P + 1 clocks after the last PER write. With RS_WR off, a period shorter than the count so far gives its first pulse only after the 20-bit count wraps, up to 2^20 clocks (about 21 ms at 50 MHz) late.

Timing rule

  • Timer: 20 bits, counts clocks. A clock with count 0 follows reset, clear, a selected restart event and the first clock of a pulse. The count rises to P - 1 and stays there (the pulse is due); the pulse starts in the next clock in which it is free: no pulse or gap running (p_hold low), no tx_act, idle_ok high, no tx_end in that clock. So the first pulse clock is P clocks after a clock with count 0, pulses repeat every P clocks, and every W + 1 clocks when P <= W (two pulses never touch). After reset the timer counts even while off, so P written later gives the first pulse P clocks after reset (the old transmitter's 800,000th clock with P = 800,000). The compare is an equality: a P written below the running count waits for the 20-bit wrap; RS_WR avoids that.
  • Pulse: p_drv is high for W clocks with p_val = VAL. p_drv is 0 in every clock with tx_act, and a frame that starts during a pulse ends it (p_hold falls one clock later).
  • Gap: tx_end sets p_hold for exactly N clocks from the next clock (a pulse in progress ends; a second tx_end starts the N clocks again).
  • p_hold = a pulse or a gap is running; it is low for at least one clock between two runs, so a frame that waits for it always gets a clock to start. A frame start and a pulse start in the same clock: the frame wins (the pulse is cut by tx_act).
  • Units: P counts clocks, not half clocks: 20 bits of clocks reach 20.97 ms at 50 MHz, which holds a 16 ms +- 8 ms period; half clocks would need 21 bits.

Connection in pe_su (sub3)

Between frames (after t_end, not act) pe_su drives the lanes with p_val instead of pv_end while p_drv is high (the pad rule stays in pe_su), and holds a frame start (start_m) while p_hold is high. The old transmitter's count was 0 in the first clock of the fixed-lane tail after a frame; with RS_TX this module's count is 0 in the clock after tx_end. If tx_end is the last clock of the tail (T clocks long), the first pulse after a frame comes T clocks later than before; to keep the old clock exactly, either pe_su gives tx_end in the clock before the tail (and the gap then counts from there), or the old timing is accepted as T clocks later (300 ns in 16 ms for the old setting).

  • Transmit: while fb_tx is on, ETH_TX (and a hart's etx) starts a frame of the unit from buffer word TXBASE, and the old line units do not start; ETH_W fills the buffer as before. The unit's pins, timing and line code are its own registers (the owner hart sets them).
  • Receive: while fb_rx is on, ETH_RXC (and erxon) arms the unit instead of the old receivers; the received words go to the buffer from word 0, and the frame ends at the unit's receive frame end, so receive in line mode uses EDGE emode 2 or 3. ETH_RXS then reports {receiving, done, crc ok, 0, 0, len[10:8]}, len[7:0] of the unit; the bytes are read with CAP_R as before.
  • The receive store ends where the transmit area begins: buffer words 0 to TXBASE - 1, the whole buffer (64 words, 128 bytes) with TXBASE 0; a frame longer than that keeps counting rx_len and its CRC. An odd last byte is written at the frame end with a 0 low byte (test_su_linemode_rx_store; RX-STORE128 and RX-FLUSH of the 11-26 re-map, the old limit was 64 bytes).
  • Line setting 14 TAIL {on, raw entry bits 6..0}: after the frame's last entry (the last CRC entry, the last buffer byte without CRC, or the immediate byte) the feeder pushes one raw entry (lane values held for 1 to 32 units, as RAW), so a frame can end on a set line state (test_su_linemode_tail; LM-TAIL of the 11-26 re-map, the 10BASE-T TP_IDL and a USB EOP).
  • A received word waits while the buffer's write port is busy (fb_wbusy, the shared memory's write queue full); the next word replaces a word still waiting, so the queue must take a word within one word time. The shared memory's four-word queue with urgent priority does (pe_shm.v header: one word every 8 clocks at the fastest line rate), as for the old line units. The su_line proof checks every word that reaches the port against the receive area (L4, L4b).
  • The CRC takes a byte in two clocks, four bits a clock in line order (the first four in the clock that takes the byte), twice the fastest line rate in sight (two lanes at one bit a clock, a byte every 4 clocks), so the feeder and the receive keep every byte in the CRC (test_line_rx_crc_byte_every_4_clocks: four lanes at one-clock units, a byte every 4 clocks; 31.5 B2, before 2026-11-26 the absorber took 5 clocks a byte and missed bytes at that rate).
  • Done holds the frame: the unit stores received bytes, counts rx_len and runs the CRC only while the receive is armed (input rx_on, in the chip su_rxarm), so a later frame on the line changes neither the buffer nor ETH_RXS until the next ETH_RXC (test_su_linemode_rx_done_holds; RX-GATE of the 11-26 re-map, fixed after the line mode was pushed).
  • A frame that a frame rule drops (G10, page 2 module 1) does not end the receive: it is not stored, ETH_RXS stays receiving and not done, and the next frame starts over at buffer word 0. An address filter is one rule line: len 6 on bytes 0 to 5 against the station address in slots 0 to 5, neg, drop and rearm (and ones to also take an all-ones address) (test_su_linemode_rx_filter).
  • One unit per command: the old line units are gated by fb_tx and fb_rx, so ETH_TX or ETH_RXC never runs two units, and only one drives a pin (test_su_linemode_one_unit).
  • The switch waits for the line: while an old line unit sends or any receive is armed (its receiver, the Protocol Block's, or the write queue), a LINE_W (and a hart's write of LCFG) is not taken and sets cfg_late (input cfg_hold), so a command is never switched to the Protocol Block under a running old unit (test_su_linemode_switch_waits; review of 78b5b8c8).
  • Evidence (test/test_su_linemode.py, chip pins and host commands): six bytes sent through LINE_W, ETH_W and ETH_TX show on P4 bit for bit; a frame received with EDGE emode 3 gives ETH_RXS done, length 6 and the bytes in the buffer; with the USB unit selected and the Protocol Block on its pin, fb_tx on runs only the Protocol Block and off only the USB unit. Controls lm_* in test/controls.sh.

Start pattern and quiet watch (page 3, G6)

  • Start pattern (sp_on): every bit group the RX pipeline receives (after NRZI, before destuffing; one bit with one lane, the lane values with two or four) goes into a 12-bit history, newest at bit 0. Words are built only after the sample at which (history ^ SPV) & SPM == 0; that sample and everything before it are not data. The match is undone by an RX flush, a frame end (fr_end) and sp_on low; a new match is then needed. It works in every receive mode (clocked, device, pair_rx, EDGE emode 3), so a start delimiter of up to 12 bits (or six two-lane groups) is a setting. Write the pattern so that its first bit on the line is the highest compared bit.
  • Quiet watch (QIET n, 1 to 255): FLAG quiet (bit 7, write 1 to clear) is set when lane rxp has kept its level for n clocks in a row, inside or outside a frame (a link that went silent). With the input synchronizer and the retiming flip-flop the flag rises n + 4 clocks after the line's last change (test_su_g6: n = 40, 44 clocks; n + 3 = 43 before the retiming, 21645a1d). The count runs from the last change or from the time QIET was written non-zero (it is held at 0 while QIET is 0), whichever is later: writing n on a line that is already quiet sets the flag n clocks later, and lowering QIET below the running count sets it in the next clock. While the line stays quiet the condition holds, so a clear is followed by the flag again in the next clock: raise QIET (or wait for a change) before clearing.
  • Evidence (test/test_su_g6.py, chip pins): a 9-bit pattern on the pair, a wrong pattern opens nothing, the noise and the pattern are not data, a flush needs a new match; the quiet flag stays 0 with a change every 40 clocks and rises 44 clocks after the last one. Controls: g6_* in test/controls.sh.

Edge-resynchronized sampling (EDGE emode 3, G5-b)

A receive mode for line codes whose bits are levels and whose edges fall on bit boundaries (NRZ, NRZI; any rate the unit timer can describe). A bit is one unit of the transmit timer settings: DIVI half clocks (0 = 256, 1 = 2) plus the DIVK/DIVM fraction carry, counted on the watched lane's two samples per clock (rising and falling synchronizer, as emode 1 and 2). Every edge restarts the bit at the sample that sees it, with the fraction at finit (sel 29, 30): the bits after an edge are DIVI half clocks until the fraction carries, so finit places the longer ones. The sample at DIVI / 2 whole half clocks into a bit gives the bit, the lane's level (inv applies).

What the restart buys is tolerance of a clock difference (a rate offset between the sender and the chip): every edge puts the sampling back in the middle of the bit. It is not a jitter filter: the edge that restarts the bit is trusted as it is, so a displaced edge moves the samples of the bits after it. In the board-trial prediction of sub2 on 17af48b9 (MORNING_RETEST_20261126/G5B_BOARD_TRIAL_DRAFT.md, section 10; restart on against restart off): a sender 1 % fast was received 15 of 15 with the restart and 0 of 15 without, but with every edge moved by up to +-27.8 ns the restart received 0 and no restart 5 (one edge in four moved: 3 to 6 against 6). Read the USB results below as rate-offset results.

  • Hunting and the end are those of emode 2 on these bits: ECNT n alternating bits and then a repeated one end the sync (the repeated bit is not data); ENDGAP half clocks without an edge, or a frame-rule rearm (one clock later, see Frame rules), end the frame. MIDMIN, MIDWIN and glitch are not used. STAT bit 0, FLAG fend, fr_start and fr_end behave as in emode 2.
  • The data bits go through the RX pipeline: SMP nrzi, STUF rx destuffing (the sync bits also advance the NRZI reference and the stuffing run, so a run that starts in the sync counts) and words of rxlen bits in the CTRL bit order. An unfinished word at the frame end is dropped, and a bit sampled in the clock of the frame end is not data (it neither completes the last word nor starts the next frame's first one).
  • Choose ENDGAP longer than the longest edge-free run of the data plus half a bit, or the end comes inside the frame.
  • A two-lane line state (for example both lanes low) is not a bit: the frame rules' lane rule with rearm ends the frame on it.
  • Example, USB full speed at 50 MHz (test test_su_usb_rx, against the old receiver on the same waveform): DIVI 8, DIVK 1, DIVM 3, finit 0, ECNT 5, ENDGAP 100, SMP 0x80, STUF 0x56, lane rule LPAT 0x23 (both lanes low, rearm), LRUN 3.
  • What is shown, and only that: test_su_usb_rx is one setting on one lane with fixed seeds; with a rate offset of +-0.25 % and 18 ns of injected uniform edge jitter the bytes of five packets equal the old receiver's and the bytes sent; 20 ns is where a run was seen to fail, an observation, not a certified limit. It does not show the whole USB receiver range, malformed packets and reacquisition, or CRC and frame-state equivalence with the old unit. test_su_rxs (pe_su alone) checks the sample times against the rule above for DIVI 0, 1, 2, 3, 10 and three fractions, with the edge seen first by either sample, and back-to-back frames whose end falls in every phase of the bit (also in the clock of a sample).

Lane pair and symbol link (G12, SLNK)

Four switches on page 2 sel 27, all 0 after reset and clear. They name no protocol: the pair is a two-lane line code, the link joins the symbol layer below to the unit's bit engine.

  • pair_tx (table mode, one segment a bit: W01 = W11 = 0): lane txp carries the table level as with one lane; lane txp + 1 changes at each bit start where lane txp keeps its level, so exactly one of the two lanes changes per bit. Both lanes keep their levels between frames (lane txp does not return to the idle level, which would read as a bit); do not use rel_idle.
  • pair_rx: a bit is lane rxp, sampled when lane rxp XOR lane rxp + 1 changes, at any rate below one change a clock (two synchronizer flip-flops, as every input). The engine's own strobes are not used, a frame start does not restart the word, and a partial word waits for more bits or an RX flush. Use it with EDGE emode 0. Both lanes are sampled once a clock after the synchronizers and a bit is taken when their XOR changed since the last clock, so two changes in one clock cancel: the time between consecutive changes (bit period minus the skew between the lanes) must be at least one clock. Measured (test_su_pair, 50 MHz): exact at every start phase down to a 20 ns spacing (period 20 ns without skew, 28 ns with 8 ns of skew either way), bits lost at 18 ns. A change of both lanes in one clock is not a bit; the code goes on from the new levels (the bit lane then reads inverted). That is a limit, not a decoding: the unit cannot tell which of the two changes was the bit, so the received word is wrong from there on. It is not silent: SLNK bit 4 (pdbl, sticky, write 1 to clear) is set, so the firmware can drop the frame (test_su_pair_double_flag: set by both lanes in one clock, 0 when the lanes change one clock or more apart; board bundle 3b reproduced the silent wrong words before the flag, 11-26; on the board with the flag, 40_BOARD_EVIDENCE_20261119/b3b4_a84646a5_20261126_2341: PA4 fast, both directions, k 1 to 8, 4 runs each, pdbl = 1 exactly when bits were lost, no silent wrong word, the board-skew loss at forward k 3 flagged 4 of 4 [measured]). Lane rxp is the bit lane: swapped lanes read the other lane's levels. The pair's bits do not hold a frame start and are not part of idle (STAT bit 6, SYNC): with pair_rx, idle can be 1 while a word is on its way into the RX FIFO, so the firmware reads words (or rqc) and does not take idle as the end of what the pair received.
  • sym_rx: the received one-lane bits (after NRZI and destuffing) go to the symbol layer, not into words; each symbol it gives out is one RX FIFO entry, bits 7..0 in RXD, bits 9..8 in RXH. Turning sym_rx on and an RX flush restart the layer's history (s_sync).
  • sym_tx: whenever no line-mode frame is being fed (fb_busy 0; with line mode unused that is always) and the TX FIFO has room, each bit the layer shows is pushed into the TX FIFO as a one-bit TXD entry (a hart push in that clock stalls). It does not wait for a frame boundary of the engine: the bits join the frame on the line, or start one. The line runs while the layer has bits (queue or fill) and stops when it has none.

Evidence (test/test_su_sym.py, from the uio pins): bytes through the pair both ways with no symbol layer (the end condition of G12: the pair is a lane function); symbols out through the layer and the pair bit for bit as the generic model of test_pe_sym.py (settings g1, not SpaceWire); the model's bit stream driven on the pair at 73 ns a bit back as the model's symbols.

Symbol layer pe_sym (G12, PE_SU_ABSORB_PLAN 28.4; linked to core 0 by SLNK)

src/pe_sym.v sits after the bit engine: on receive it cuts the bit stream into symbols, checks a parity bit and drops chosen symbols; on transmit it turns queued symbols, or a fill symbol when the queue is empty, into bits with the same rules. Every length, range, value and polarity is a register (page 2 window, XIDX[7:6] = 2), all 0 after reset and clear (off). The header of src/pe_sym.v has the exact rules; in short:

  • Frame on the line: [P] then the symbol bits s[0] .. s[len-1]. A symbol value has s[0] in bit 0 and zeros at and above its length (sy_d, queue entries, FA/FB, DA/DB).
  • Length: with a marker, s[mpos] (or {s[mpos+1], s[mpos]}) picks LEN[v]; without, LEN[0]. A length is raised to the marker end and to 1, and cut to 10.
  • Parity: P makes the XOR of P, the previous symbol's bits i >= pf and this symbol's bits i < pc equal par_odd. History restarts as zeros at a transmit start and at s_sync.
  • Receive hold: when the parity covers the previous symbol (pf < 10) or a pair is dropped, a symbol is given out only when the next one has completed (it is checked by the next P).
  • Drop: same length and equal on DMASK bits; one symbol (DA) or a pair at symbol boundaries (DA then DB). A parity error discards the held symbol, gives sy_err and stops the receiver until s_sync.
  • Transmit: show-ahead (st_bit_v, st_bit; st_req takes the bit), no gap between frames; a fill unit (FA, or FA FB) is never split by a queued symbol; queue of two entries.
  • Units and classes (TXPRI v2, 4a): the line takes units of one or two symbols, and a two-symbol unit is never split (its second symbol is the next load). At a unit boundary the next unit comes from the class of the best rank (CLS) with a ready unit: class H (one unit of one or two symbols, ready only when both are written), class M (one unit of one symbol), class N (the queue, one symbol each); fill only when none has one. The SpaceWire setting is H = time-code (ESC and its data character), M = FCT, N = N-Chars, fill = Null, which is the order of ECSS-E-ST-50-12C 5.5.6 a to e. The queue starves only while H or M always has a ready unit at a boundary; that is a load assumption on the firmware (one FCT per eight N-Chars, rare time-codes), not a hardware limit.
