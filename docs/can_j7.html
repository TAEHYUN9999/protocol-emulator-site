Simulation only (RTL, Icarus Verilog 11.0, cocotb 2.0.1). Chip A runs firmware/can_gen.pe (can.md) and sends frames from its host FIFO; chip B runs firmware/can_dist.pe (can_dist.md) and drives one chosen bit of A's frames dominant for a chosen number of clocks. A's error counts (TEC, REC, STATE) are read after every error flag and compared, step by step, with test/can_rules.py, the rules of Bosch CAN 2.0 Part A section 7 written out from the text, fed with the bus events the test sees. Until now the fault confinement was tested against a cocotb node that forced the bits (can.md, test_can_fc) and the disturber against a cocotb node (can_dist.md); here both sides are the chip.

A board version (two chips, or the chip and the KD240's CAN controller as victim) is planned by the board session and not part of this page.

Bench (test/tb2.v)

                     120 ns (transport)
chip A P0 (TXD) -----[ delay ]-----\
chip B P0 (TXD) -----[ delay ]------ AND ---- can_bus ---+--> chip A P1 (RXD)
cocotb txd_m (model node) ---------/                     +--> chip B P1 (RXD)
                                                         +--> cocotb (ACK node, recorder)
clk (20 ns) --> both chips          rst_n, ena, ui_in (host SPI) per chip: _a, _b
  • Two instances of tt_um_taehyun9999_protocol_emulator (chip_a, chip_b), one clock, each with its own reset, enable and host SPI pins, so cocotb loads and runs each one alone. The RTL in src/ is not changed.
  • The transceiver is the one of the one-chip CAN tests (CanBench in test/test_can_gen.py): TXD reaches the bus 120 ns later, every edge kept; the bus reaches RXD at once; dominant = 0. The bus is the wired AND of both delayed TXDs and txd_m, the TXD of a cocotb node. A pin a chip does not drive floats high (pull-up), as in test/tb.v.
  • test/Makefile takes the bench from TB_TOP (default tb, so every other module runs as before): make -B NODUMP=1 TB_TOP=tb2 COCOTB_TEST_MODULES=test_can_j7. tools/ci_shards.py has the same arguments in its table of runs.
  • In the test, ChipView gives each chip the signal names that Bench and CanBench use on tb.v, so the host link, the host FIFO task, the record split and load() of test_can_gen.py and load_dist() of test_can_dist.py are reused unchanged. Both chips at 250 kbit/s (100 ticks per bit), sample point 75 %, SJW 16 ticks: the bus of test_can_gen.py.
  • The frame is the one of test_can_dist.py: standard ID 0x123, data F7 3C, 52 stuffed bits SOF to CRC. B disturbs bus bit 21 (SOF = 0, stuff bits counted), the second data bit, recessive after a recessive bit, so the pulse starts with an edge and A sends recessive there.
  • The cocotb node only acknowledges: it hard-syncs on SOF, samples at 75 % of its bit, destuffs, checks the CRC and drives the ACK slot of a good frame. It sends no error flag (a frame that breaks off with six equal bits is dropped), so the only flags on the bus are A's, and only A's counts change. B does not acknowledge either.
  • CAN_J7_FW selects another firmware file for chip A (the controls below), CAN_DIST_FW one for chip B.

Tests

Test Checks
test_can_j7_tec B hits bit 21 of every frame of A with 150 to 166 clocks (one more per frame; 3.0 to 3.3 us of the 4 us bit). After each attempt, 10 bits after bit 21, A's counts by DATA_R against the rules fed with one transmit bit error (rule 3): 17 errors, flag active up to the 16th (rule 9: the error that reaches 128 is still signalled active) and passive at the 17th. B stops after its 17 lengths; A's retransmission and 8 more frames get the ACK, each read after EOF: TEC - 1 per frame (rule 7), error active again at 127 (rule 11). B loaded again: 17 more errors, active flag at 127, passive after, bus off at the 17th (TEC 256 or more, rule 10). Then: no TXD edge of A until it sends again, which must be within -1 to +3 bits of the end of the 128th sequence of 11 recessive bits counted from the recorded bus (rule 12), with TEC 0, REC 0, error active. Also: one B pulse per attempt of exactly the programmed length; B's map of seen flags = 1 for A's active flags and 0 for its passive ones; A's flag on its TXD from bit 22 for an active flag, none for a passive one; record status 0x85 per error, 0x81 per good frame; no late flag on either chip
test_can_j7_sample_point B lengthens its pulse on bit 21 by one clock per frame from D_FIRST - 17 clocks (120), and stops after 4 flags in a row. Per attempt: A's flag on the bus (an active flag from bit 22) decides which event the rules get, bit error (+8) or good frame (-1 unless 0); A's counts must follow. Every shorter pulse must leave the frame good (status 0x81, TEC unchanged at 0), every longer one up to B's stop must give exactly one bit error at bit 21 (status 0x85, the record ends after the 20 destuffed bits before it, TEC + 8). B's map and threshold must equal A's verdicts. The first length with an error must be D_FIRST, computed from the configured sample point (below), B's pulse must start where that computation assumes, and the bracket of the measured readback must hold the sample point counted from A's TXD edge

The first length with an error, from the settings: as a transmitter, can_gen.pe keeps its own bit grid (it does not resynchronize while sending, can.md, "What fits") and judges a bit it sends by one read of RXD, SP + 1 ticks after the instruction that drives TXD, 76 ticks here (can.md, "Transmit: readback at the sample point"). Through the two-flop pin synchronizer (src/project.v) that read takes the bus level of 2 clocks before it: 76 x 2 - 2 = 150 clocks = SP after A's TXD edge (3000 ns). B's pulse reaches the bus 14 clocks after that edge (A's edge reaches the bus 6 clocks, 120 ns, later and B starts 8 clocks after that), so a pulse of D clocks covers the read when 14 + D > 150: D_FIRST = 2 x 75 - 14 + 1 = 137 clocks. A pulse of 136 clocks ends on the clock edge at which the read is taken; it reads recessive in this simulation (the bus change of that time step reaches the input flop after it sampled, as for the 102-clock pulse of the earlier run below). Counted from A's bit start at the bus, the read is at 3000 - 120 = 2880 ns, 72 % of the bit: the configured 75 % less the transceiver loop delay, which a transmitter that counts its sample point from its own TXD edge does not see. A node that resynchronizes on B's edge as Bosch 2.0 Part A section 8 rule 4 asks of a node sending recessive (the model node of can_dist.md) would put its sample point SP after that edge, and the first length with an error would be floor(3000 ns / 20 ns) + 1 = 151 clocks: the 14 clocks between 137 and 151 are B's offset from A's TXD edge, which such a node would not count.

Until 2026-11-18 A read the bus twice, one tick apart, at SP - SJW - 1 and SP - SJW ticks after its bit start (58 and 59 ticks; the second decided), so it judged the bus level at 59 x 2 - 2 = 116 clocks (2320 ns) after its TXD edge, 2200 ns after that edge reached the bus: 55 % of the bit, and the first length with an error was 103 clocks (the measurement of that firmware is kept below).

Limits

  • Simulation only, on the RTL with Icarus Verilog; not at gate level. Not run on a board; the board version (two chips, or the chip as disturber against the KD240's CAN controller) is planned by the board session.
  • One clock drives both chips, so there is no frequency offset between them: A's grid and B's bit follower never drift apart, and the ACK node samples at a fixed 75 % without resynchronizing. Rate offsets are tested in the one-chip tests (can.md, test_can_gen_rates).
  • The ACK node sends no error flags and B sends none, so A's flags are never overlapped by others' flags: rule 6 (the 8th dominant bit after a flag) and the receive counts (REC stayed 0) are not exercised here; test_can_fc_receive_errors covers them against a cocotb node. Rules 4 and 5 and exception 2 are not done in can_gen.pe (can.md, Fault confinement) and not exercised.
  • B can only add dominant level (a transceiver cannot force recessive), so only recessive bits of A can be hit; the bit is chosen by its index in the stuffed frame, so the frame content is fixed in the test.
  • The measured monitoring point (SP after A's own TXD edge, 72 % of the bit at the bus with the 120 ns loop) is how can_gen.pe behaves as a transmitter: no resynchronization while sending, and the readback at the configured sample point counted from its own bit start (can.md, Timing). A transmitter that follows section 8 rule 4 would resynchronize on B's edge and sample SP after it (151 clocks here). The test checks the firmware against the settings and the rules model; it does not claim that this monitoring point is what the standard asks for.
  • A pulse that ends exactly on A's sampling clock edge (136 clocks now, 102 before the change) is decided by the simulator's event order, not by a modelled setup and hold time.
