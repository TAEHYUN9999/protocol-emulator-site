firmware/w25q.pe The chip answers as a Winbond W25Q128JV serial flash on its pins: JEDEC ID, status register 1 with BUSY and WEL, write enable and disable, read and fast read, page program, sector erase and chip erase, with program and erase times set by the host. Hart 0 decodes the instructions and answers on the bus; hart 1 keeps BUSY for the set time and erases the array window. The array window is the 128-byte frame buffer. 349 of 512 (hart 1 starts at word 311)

This protocol was not part of the design when the RTL was written. It uses only existing instructions (waitp.to with tset, shin, shout, rel, ld/st to the frame buffer and the data memory, tsetr/twait on hart 1); the RTL is unchanged. Checked in simulation only, not on a board.

Section numbers below are those of the Winbond W25Q128JV datasheet, Revision F (March 27, 2018).

Pins

Pin Use
uio[2] (P2) CLK input
uio[3] (P3) DI input
uio[4] (P4) DO, driven only while an answer is being shifted out, released otherwise
uio[5] (P5) /CS input

The same pins as spi_device.pe (the board mapping). A pull-up on DO makes a released DO read FFh, as it does on a board with a real flash and a pull-up.

Array window

Flash bytes 000000h to 00007Fh are the frame buffer: flash byte a is load/store address 0x20 + a, frame byte a. The host fills it with ETH_W (0x40, word k holds bytes 2k and 2k+1) and reads it with CAP_R (0x32), as for the Ethernet frames; the capture and the Ethernet transmitter and receiver must be idle while the emulation runs. Every other flash address reads FFh, and programs and erases there change nothing visible (BUSY still runs for the set time). A page program to page 0 ANDs into the window; the bytes for offsets 80h to FFh of page 0 are dropped.

Linux spi-nor (v5.15 drivers/mtd/spi-nor/winbond.c, line 93) lists EF4018h as "w25q128" with SECT_4K and no dual or quad flag, so it does not read SFDP and erases 4 KB sectors with 20h. The emulation therefore does not need an SFDP table. A Linux MTD device on it would have 16 MB of which the first 128 bytes are real.

Host interface and settings

Data memory, written by the host with DATA_W before the run:

Address Use
0xA0 to 0xA3 tPP: P (ticks, high byte, low byte), N (high, low)
0xA4 to 0xA7 tSE: P, N
0xA8 to 0xAB tCE: P, N
0x00 Status register 1, read with DATA_R at any time (bit 0 BUSY, bit 1 WEL)
0x01 Hart 0 to hart 1: the operation in progress
0xFE FFh, written by hart 1 at the start (read pointer outside the window)

Busy time = P x N ticks of 40 ns, counted from the moment hart 0 sees /CS high (see Timing for how late that can be). P must be at least 8 ticks (the count loop takes 6), N = 0 counts as 65536. Erasing the window (sector 0 or chip erase) adds 15.4 us (128 stores) before the count starts. Examples: P 2500 (100 us), N 4 gives the typical tPP of 0.4 ms; N 450 the typical tSE of 45 ms; P 50000 (2 ms), N 20000 the typical tCE of 40 s. Soft reset keeps the data memory, so the settings stay for the next run.

The host FIFO is not used.

Timing

One tick is 40 ns. Hart 0 sees a pin through two synchronizer flip-flops and only in its own cycle, so it reacts to a CLK edge 2 to 3 clocks after it and needs one more tick to act. It reads DI one tick after it sees the rising edge, and it changes DO one tick after it sees the falling edge. The longest stretches of code after a rising edge, before the next bit is ready or the next falling edge is waited for, are about 10 to 13 ticks: the decode after the instruction byte (9Fh and 05h answer in the next bit), the sort after the first address byte, the read pointer after the last address byte of 03h, and the pointer set-up in bit 7 of the first data byte of 02h. They decide the clock limit.

Measured with test_w25q_max_sck (mode 0, the host model reads DO at the rising edge with no setup margin of its own and changes DI at the falling edge; each step boots the firmware again, then 9Fh with 3 bytes, 05h with 3 bytes, 03h at 000000h (8 bytes) and 00007Ch (6 bytes, across the window end), 0Bh at 000020h (8 bytes), and 06h, 02h with 4 data bytes, a read back and a CAP_R check). "DO after fall" is the latest DO change after a falling edge over the whole step, "margin" the shortest time from a DO change to the next rising edge:

Fastest CLK with every instruction working: 1.79 MHz (9Fh alone up to 2.27 MHz). At 1.79 MHz the last DO change comes 60 ns before the sampling edge, so a real master with its own input setup time and board delays will need a slower clock; at the KD240's 585.9 kHz the margin is 420 ns. The earlier skeleton of this firmware (review E) passed at 700 kHz and failed at 800 kHz. Two changes moved the limit: the instruction is sorted out after the first address byte instead of before it (the old chain of compares took about 27 ticks after the last instruction bit, more than one CLK period at 1 MHz, and a PC trace at 1 MHz showed it missing the first address bit of 20h and 02h), and TMO is 32 ticks instead of 16 (with 16, the 7-tick look at /CS fell on the next falling edge for half periods of about 9 to 12 ticks; 1.25 MHz failed that way).

/CS (test_w25q_cs_timing, 1 MHz):

Datasheet Measured
DO released after /CS rises tSHQZ at most 7 ns 1154 to 1169 ns (four 05h frames)
/CS high between frames tSHSL1 at least 10 ns (read), tSHSL2 50 ns (erase, program) 1.2 us and longer work; 1.0 us and shorter fail (05h, 9Fh and 03h frames back to back)

Both come from TMO: the firmware notices /CS high only when a wait for a CLK level has timed out, 32 ticks plus the 7-tick look.

DI hold (test_w25q_di_hold, 1 MHz, the host changes DI the given time after the rising edge instead of at the falling edge): DI changed 100, 120, 150 and 200 ns after the rising edge works; 80, 60 and 40 ns fail (the instruction is misread, so 9Fh is not answered). The firmware needs DI held 100 ns after the rising edge. The datasheet asks the master for 2 ns (tCHDX); masters in mode 0 and 3 that change DI at the falling edge hold it for half a period.

Busy times (test_w25q_ops, 585.9 kHz, the first status byte with BUSY = 0 counted from the /CS rise of the instruction): tPP set to 400 us read ready after 420.2 us; tSE set to 1000 us after 1028.3 us (sector 1) and 1042.0 us (sector 0, with the 15.4 us window erase); tCE set to 2000 us after 2041.0 us. One status byte takes 13.7 us at this clock, so the excess is the polling step plus the time to see /CS high.

Every wait for a CLK level has its own timeout handler that returns to the same wait (49 words); that is what lets a slow master pause anywhere and still lets the firmware see /CS rise.

Tests

test/test_w25q.py (cocotb). The host model and the flash model are written from the datasheet, not from the firmware: the host is an SPI master in mode 0 or 3 that changes DI at the falling edge of CLK and reads DO at the rising edge with no extra delay, and records every DO edge and the chip's DO output enable; the flash model keeps the window, WEL and BUSY and gives for every frame the expected DO bytes and whether DO may be driven. Checked for every frame: the DO bytes, DO released during instruction, address and input bytes and for ignored instructions, no DO change while CLK is high (9.7: DO changes after a falling edge), status bytes against the BUSY interval (BUSY and WEL during the set time, then ready; a first ready byte must not be followed by a busy one), and the window (CAP_R) against the model after programs and erases. Only the pins, the window mapping and the busy time settings come from this document. Run from a private directory:

T=<repo>/test; R=$T/sim_build/<name>; mkdir -p $R; cd $R
PYTHONPATH=$T make -B -f $T/Makefile PWD=$T SIM_BUILD=$R/build COCOTB_TEST_MODULES=test_w25q

One run of the whole module on 2026-11-11: TESTS=6 PASS=6 FAIL=0, 91.5 ms of simulated time, 859 s of wall time on a loaded machine (COCOTB_TEST_FILTER runs one test).

Controls

Each change is a text substitution of w25q.pe made by the test when W25Q_MUTANT names it (the MUTANTS table in test_w25q.py); with a mutant the test stops at the first difference. Run 2026-11-11 with test_w25q_ops, all five fail:

Control Change Caught by (test_w25q_ops)
m1_no_wel_check 02h does not check WEL 02h without WEL started a program: status 03h where 00h is due
m2_store_not_and the page program stores the data byte instead of ANDing it 03h after the program reads A5h where A5h AND the old byte (A0h) is due
m3_wel_kept hart 1 leaves WEL set at the end of the busy time status 02h after BUSY cleared, 00h due
m4_id_while_busy 9Fh not ignored while BUSY DO driven for 9Fh during tPP
m5_no_byte_boundary /CS rising inside a data byte commits the page program the program cut in its first data byte started (status 03h, 02h due)

Limits

  • The array is 128 bytes (000000h to 00007Fh); the rest of the 16 MB reads FFh and cannot be programmed. A Linux MTD device on it would see 16 MB.
  • Data is ANDed while it arrives (one byte late). A page program cut by /CS inside a data byte does not start (BUSY and WEL stay, as 8.2.13 asks), but the whole data bytes before the cut one are already in the window (test_w25q_ops checks this documented behaviour). More than 256 data bytes AND again instead of replacing the earlier ones in the page buffer.
  • A sector erase with any CLK rise after its last address byte (one bit is tested; a whole fifth byte goes the same way) is not executed; the datasheet only says /CS must rise after the eighth bit of the last byte.
  • 06h and 04h take effect when /CS rises right after their 8 bits; with more clocks they are not executed (the datasheet does not say).
  • A read does not wrap from FFFFFFh to 000000h.
  • DO is released only when the firmware sees /CS high, up to TMO (32 ticks, 1.28 us) plus the look later (see Timing), not within tSHQZ (7 ns). On a bus with other SPI devices the next device must not drive MISO within that time.
  • /CS must stay high longer than the firmware needs to see it (see Timing); the datasheet asks only 10 ns (tSHSL1) or 50 ns (tSHSL2).
  • DO changes up to 434 ns after the falling edge of CLK (measured at 585.9 kHz; 180 to 220 ns at 0.8 to 1.8 MHz), not within tCLQV (6 ns); the first output bit of an instruction comes about ten ticks after the rising edge that completes the instruction or address. This, not the datasheet's 50 or 133 MHz, sets the clock limit of 1.79 MHz.
  • DI must be held 100 ns after the rising edge (measured; the datasheet allows the master a 2 ns hold). Masters in mode 0 and 3 change DI at the falling edge.
  • Busy time resolution is one P; the time starts when hart 0 sees /CS high. Window erases add 15.4 us.
  • Not tested: Linux on a board, AXI Quad SPI co-simulation, gate-level simulation.
