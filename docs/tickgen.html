An FPGA's clock manager (MMCM) makes a clock of any frequency with a fractional divider. This chip has one 50 MHz clock and no PLL, so the same job is done with a clock enable: each hart gets src/pe_tickgen.v, a generator of ticks at fclk / (N + k/M), and an instruction twait.f that waits for the next tick. A firmware can then send and sample at any baud rate or audio rate with a mean error of zero, where the hart timer (tset/twait) can only use whole 40 ns ticks. Branch int6u developed and verified the generator and the instruction model on their own; branch int6_tick connects them to pe_core.v of int5f f5f5660, one generator per hart (see Integration).

Numbers are labelled [measured] (a run of the tests or tools named next to them), [calculated] (arithmetic on measured or specified values) or [estimate].

Definition

Period i (i = 1, 2, ... after a start) lasts

N + floor(i k / M) - floor((i - 1) k / M) clocks,

so with the fraction on (0 < k < M):

  • every period is N or N + 1 clocks;
  • any w consecutive periods hold floor(w k / M) or one more long periods;
  • any M consecutive periods take exactly M N + k clocks: the mean is N + k/M with no error;
  • tick i ends at edge i N + floor(i k / M) after the start, between one clock before the ideal i (N + k/M) and the ideal itself. The error never accumulates.

With k = 0 or k >= M the fraction is off and every period is N clocks. N is 16 bits (1 to 65,535 clocks, down to 763 Hz; N = 0 stops), k and M 12 bits: every rate fclk / f with f in Hz whose reduced denominator is at most 4,095 is exact, which covers the standard serial and audio rates (table below).

When the target is not a fraction with M up to 4,095, tools/tickgen_model.best_nkm picks the closest one (continued fractions); the error is then a fixed ppm, still without jitter growth: it accumulates only as that ppm times the elapsed time.

Circuit

The same as the shift unit's unit timer (src/pe_su.v on branch su): a down counter with a last flag, and the carry as a one-clock extension at the end of a period (xc), so no adder follows the compare. At the end of a period the accumulator becomes acc + k or acc + k - M (carry) and xc takes the carry for the next period. Unlike the SU the generator keeps no M - k register (the first version did: 86 flip-flops, 9,053 um^2; this one 72 and 7,734 um^2, see Area).

Interface

Port Width Meaning
clear 1 soft reset: stopped (N = 0), k = M = 0, flags cleared
run 1 the hart runs; the generator counts only then (frozen otherwise, like the hart timer)
wr, wsel, wdata 1, 2, 16 wsel 0: N = wdata and start; 1: k = wdata[11:0]; 2: M = wdata[11:0]
take 1 twait.f ends in this clock: one pending tick is used
tick 1 high in the last clock of each period
ready 1 a tick is pending or the generator is stopped
late 1 sticky: a tick came while one was still pending, so one was lost; the grid of ticks does not move

Instructions

Instruction Encoding Operation Flags Ticks
tfn ra,rb 1111 0aaa 0010 1bbb N = {ra, rb}, start: the first tick ends N clocks after this edge; N = 0 stops kept 1
tfk ra,rb 1111 0aaa 0110 1bbb k = {ra[3:0], rb} kept 1
tfm ra,rb 1111 0aaa 1010 1bbb M = {ra[3:0], rb} kept 1
twait.f 0xF0E8 wait until a tick is pending, use it kept (like twait) 1, or until the tick
twait.f.nb 0xF0E9 use a pending tick if there is one, never wait Z = 1: none was pending 1

ra sits in bits 10:8 and rb in bits 2:0, where int5's ldx/stx keep rd and ra, so pe_core already forms {r[ins[10:8]], r[ins[2:0]]} for opcode 0xF. The other words of the group (1111 0xxx 1110 1xxx but 0xF0E8 and 0xF0E9) are reserved and do nothing.

Rules:

  • Write k and M before N: they take effect at the next period end, and tfn starts the accumulator at k. A change while running can leave acc >= M for a few periods (more carries) until it falls below M.
  • tfn restarts the phase, so a receiver can align the grid to a start edge it saw, and a transmitter starts every frame on the grid.
  • A stopped generator (N = 0) is always ready, so twait.f cannot hang on a generator that was never started.
  • late belongs to the generator. In the chip it is ORed into the hart's existing late flag (STATUS byte 3, bit 3 or 7), which already means "the firmware did not keep up".

Relation to tset, twait, tpre

The generator is separate from the hart timer. twait and waitp.to keep using the timer (periods in units of 2^n ticks, timeouts, tgeth/tgetl), twait.f uses the generator, and a firmware can use both at once, for example bits on the generator and a frame timeout on the timer. Two differences in behaviour:

tset P / twait tfn / twait.f
Unit 2^n ticks of 40 ns 1 clock of 20 ns, plus k/M
Mean period P x 2^n x 40 ns exactly (N + k/M) x 20 ns exactly
A late wait ends at once and the next period starts there: the grid moves ends at once on the pending tick: the grid stays (test_late_keeps_grid)
Counts while the hart runs the hart runs (run)

Precision seen by a hart

A hart acts only at its own clock edge, every second clock, and ready is a register, so twait.f ends 1 or 2 clocks after the tick's edge. The pin edges a firmware makes after twait.f therefore lie within 2 clocks peak to peak of the ideal line (+-1 clock around its centre), and consecutive edges are N - 1 to N + 2 clocks apart, always even [measured, test_rates]. The mean stays exact. The R2 proposal's .h pin writes (one clock later, in the other hart's slot) could take the remaining clock: with the tick's phase within the hart's tick known, a firmware would choose the .h form to put each edge on the 20 ns grid [not built].

Firmware sketch (UART transmitter at 921,600 bit/s on P0)

        ldi r2, 0
        ldi r3, 73
        tfk r2, r3          ; k = 73
        ldi r2, 0x01
        ldi r3, 0x20
        tfm r2, r3          ; M = 0x120 = 288
        ldi r2, 0
        ldi r3, 54          ; N = 54
        ldi r4, 0
        high 0
tx:     rxb r0
        tfn r2, r3          ; start the grid
        twait.f             ; the start bit begins on a tick
        low 0
        ldi r1, 8
bit:    twait.f
        shout.lsb r0, 0
        djnz r1, bit
        twait.f
        high 0              ; stop bit
        tfn r4, r4          ; stop the generator until the next byte
        jmp tx

Every edge comes one tick after the twait.f that precedes it, so all ten edges of a frame are on the grid (+-1 clock); the bit loop has 54 clocks for three instructions (6 clocks). The stop bit lasts until the next start edge, at least one period, because tfn restarts the grid and the start bit waits for its first tick. tfn r4, r4 (N = 0) stops the generator while the hart waits in rxb; without it the ticks of that wait are not taken and set the hart's late flag (the first chip run of this sketch did: STATUS byte 3 = 0x08). test_tickgen_chip runs this sketch at 921,600 bit/s. The same loop with tset 27/twait runs at 54 clocks per bit, -4,672 ppm [measured, control below].

By field: ins[11] = 1 jumps; with ins[11] = 0, ins[4:3] = 00 is ldx/stx, ins[4] = 1 the SU, and ins[4:3] = 01 is shared by the delay line (ins[5] = 0, dlyw only with ins[7:5] = 100) and the tick generator (ins[5] = 1). The free 64 words are ins[7:5] = 110 with ins[4:3] = 01.

The decoder of int5f f5f5660 took the whole ins[4:3] = 01 group for the delay line and chose by ins[7] alone: there tfn and tfk were dlyr (into ra), and tfm, twait.f, twait.f.nb, the reserved and the free words were dlyw (an earlier version of this section called the R7 words no-ops for int5; they were not). int6 narrows the delay line decode to its own rows (dlyw only with ins[7:5] = 100, dlyr with ins[7] = 0 and ins[5] = 0), so the free words now do nothing. tools/tickgen_model.py has both decoders (int5_decode, int6_expect); test_alloc_0xf checks them against the table, and test_tickgen_chip.test_tick_decode_all runs all 2,048 words with ins[11] = 0 on the chip RTL, reads the operation from the decoder's control signals, and finds it equal to the table for every word [measured: 1,150 no-ops, 256 ldx, 256 stx, 128 dlyr, 64 dlyw, 64 each of tfn, tfk, tfm, one each of twait.f, twait.f.nb]. No firmware used the R7 words (test/check_asm_compat.py --rev f5f5660, below). An older proposal in RTL_NEXT.md 2.4 put the SU configuration at 1111 0sss 0001 cccc / 1111 0ddd 1001 cccc; the SU proposal replaced it with the rows above (docs/su.md on the unmerged branch su).

Formal

formal/run_formal_tickgen.sh with formal/pe_tickgen_props.sv. Every input is free (clear, run, the writes and their data, take); the assumptions are reset in the first clock and take only when ready (the core ends twait.f only then). Claims:

  1. Every period is N or N + 1 counted clocks, and exactly N when the fraction was off at the start of the period.
  2. No tick while stopped (N = 0) or frozen (run = 0), none after reset or clear until a start.
  3. ready = pending or stopped; a start clears the pending tick; otherwise the pending flag is the tick of the clock before or the older pending tick not taken (a take in the clock of a new tick keeps the new one); late is set by a tick that meets a pending one without a take and stays until clear; the pending flag never rises without a tick.
  4. After a start with the fraction on and k, M not written, acc < M (every carry decision is the exact Bresenham one).
  5. (BMC only, N up to 3, k and M up to 5, the hart running, no write after the start) the M-th tick after a start ends exactly M N + k counted clocks after it.

Sharing with the SU's fractional divider

The SU (branch su) has the same unit timer with its own k and M. Measured on pe_su.v of that branch with the same methods: 44,450 um^2 flat; with its fraction tied off 40,764 um^2, so its fraction datapath (accumulator, M - k, carry) is 3,686 um^2 and 27 flip-flops [measured]. Options:

The fraction off (integer N only) 4,246 um^2 per generator [calculated] loses the zero mean error, the point of R7: rejected
Keep both 0

Decision for int6_tick: one generator per hart, as built. The SU option saves nothing until the SU is integrated (the SU is not on this branch). One generator per core would save 15,892 um^2 but take twait.f from harts 1 and 3, which the chip test uses (hart 3 makes the I2S BCLK while hart 0 sends UART) and which a firmware needs when a transmitter and a receiver run on the two harts of one core. Integer-only generators break the zero mean error. If the layout with the generators does not close, the per-core variant is the measured fallback (synthesized above with u_tg1 replaced by a stub that is always ready). When the SU is integrated, its unit timer should be fed by its owner's generator (the definition and the circuit are already the same; the SU's test_fraction checks that definition on the SU side).

Formal in the chip

  • Hart isolation (formal/run_formal_iso.sh, two copies of pe_core): the nine registers of hart 0's generator (exposed under FORMAL as dbg_h0_tg) are claimed equal in both copies, next to the other hart-0 state. ISO_ALL PASS [measured, formal/logs/iso_20261114_143641/, 34 min]: k-induction inductive at step 3, PDR 26 s, BMC 12, the reachability control diverges, 9 of 9 planted bugs caught by the induction step, four of them on the generator (wr and take of u_tg0 without !slot, hart 0's twait.f reading hart 1's ready, u_tg0 stopped by the halt of the hart that owns the clock).
  • Power-on and stopped harts (formal/run_formal_por.sh, the whole chip): claims TICK. Before START all four generators hold their reset values; the generator of a hart that could not run in a clock (run bit 0 or halted) keeps its state across it; after a soft reset all four hold their reset values. POR_ALL PASS [measured, formal/logs/por_20261114_143729/, 61 min]: k-induction 25 s, PDR 46 s, BMC 12, the reachability control, 27 of 27 planted bugs caught, four of them new (a generator that counts while its hart is halted and a write without exec: traces of 43 clocks from reset; a soft reset that does not clear the generators: the induction step; a pending tick after reset: BMC at clock 3).
  • The same two proofs on the branch int6 RTL with its fixes (RTL revision 0x05, branch int6_rtl2, the debug unit and the SRAM self test in the chip): ISO_ALL PASS [measured, formal/logs/iso_20261114_230257/] and POR_ALL PASS [measured, formal/logs/por_20261114_230257/]. Besides the 921,600 bit/s UART sketch of test_tick_uart_921600, two protocol firmwares were rewritten onto the generator; each is a copy of the whole-tick firmware with the timer setup and the waits replaced, so the original and its evidence stay as they were:

The edges of both lie within 2 clocks peak to peak of the ideal line, the precision this page gives for any pin write after twait.f. The .h writes of docs/isa3.md could take that to one clock; neither firmware does it.

Limits

  • The chip numbers are from RTL simulation and the formal proofs; no gate-level simulation or layout includes the generators yet.
  • The hart sees a tick 1 or 2 clocks late (the register and the barrel slot): edges are +-1 clock around the ideal line, not on it. The generator's own tick is on the 20 ns grid with deviation in (-1, 0].
  • N is at most 65,535 clocks (763 Hz); slower rates count several ticks in a loop.
  • ready is a new input to the PC hold of pe_core (twait.f); no timing analysis of the core with the generators was run (the standalone figures are in Area and timing).
  • Rates whose fraction needs M > 4,095 get the best fraction below that, with a constant ppm error.
